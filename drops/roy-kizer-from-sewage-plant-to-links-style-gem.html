Roy Kizer — From Sewage Plant to Links-Style Gem

10 Pieces

Built on a decommissioned wastewater treatment plant in southeast Austin, Roy Kizer is the muni that shouldn't exist — and it might be the most interesting public course in the city. Named after the man who kept Lions Muny alive as superintendent from 1937 to 1973, the course opened in 1994 and plays like a proper links: 40-plus bunkers, 35 acres of lakes, 22 acres of wetlands, and a par-71 layout stretching 6,749 yards.

Sit it next to its sibling Jimmy Clay — a parkland track sharing the same address — and you've got 36 holes in two completely different styles for $35 weekday, $44 weekend. The GolfATX muni system doesn't get enough credit for what it offers, and Roy Kizer is Exhibit A. Southeast Austin's best-kept secret is hiding in plain sight.

The Collection — 10 Pieces

Roy Kizer Golf Course
Austin, TX · Est. 1994
Roy Kizer Golf Course sits on 200 acres of former wastewater treatment plant in southeast Austin — a landscape most cities would've turned into a parking lot or left fallow. Instead, architect Randolph Russell transformed it into one of the most interesting municipal courses in Texas. Opened in 1994, the course works with 80+ acres of existing lakes and marshland rather than fighting them. It's links-style golf on a site with a past, and that's part of what makes it worth playing.

Roy Kizer
Lions Muny superintendent, 1937–1973
The course is named after Roy Kizer, who served as golf course superintendent at Lions Municipal Golf Course from 1937 until his retirement in 1973. He died in 1974. For 36 years, Kizer kept Lions Muny — Austin's first public course — in playable condition through wars, droughts, and budget fights. He was also a passionate advocate for junior golf in Austin. Naming the new course after him connected two generations of Austin muni golf.

The Transformation
Sewage plant → links-style gem
In 1994, Randolph Russell took the decommissioned City of Austin wastewater treatment facility on Williamson Creek and turned it into something nobody expected — a links-style course with more character than most private clubs. The existing lakes and wetlands became design features, not obstacles to bulldoze. Russell's philosophy was to give pleasure to the greatest number of players regardless of skill level. The result is a course that's accessible but never boring.

Jimmy Clay Golf Course
The sibling course · Est. 1974
Jimmy Clay Golf Course is the older sibling, opened in 1974 and designed by Joe Finger. It sits at the same 5400 Jimmy Clay Drive address and offers a completely different experience — tree-lined, traditional parkland golf along Williamson Creek with elevated greens and forgiving tee shots. It was renovated in 2007 with three holes redesigned, an island-green water feature added, and all greens replanted. Together with Kizer, it forms a 36-hole complex.

The Links Experience
Par 71 · 6,749 yds · 40+ bunkers
Roy Kizer plays as a par 71 at 6,749 yards from the tips, with a course rating of 72.1 and a slope of 124. The layout is flat, wind-exposed, and dotted with more than 40 bunkers — which is why it earns the links comparison. The toughest hole is No. 11, a 468-yard par 4 that plays directly into the prevailing south wind. Five sets of tees make it playable for everyone, but the wind makes it a different course every day.

Wetlands & Wildlife
35 acres of lakes · 22 acres of wetlands
The ecological story at Kizer is as compelling as the golf. The course contains 35 acres of lakes and 22 acres of constructed wetlands that serve as habitat for resident and migratory waterfowl. It's listed as a birding hotspot on BirdingHotspots.org. You'll see herons, egrets, and hawks while you play. The course effectively serves dual purposes — public recreation and urban wildlife habitat. Not many munis can say that.

36 Holes, Two Worlds
Links vs. parkland at one address
At the same address, you get two completely different golf experiences. Kizer is links-style — open, windy, bunker-heavy, with water on multiple holes and mounding that frames every fairway. Jimmy Clay is parkland — tree-lined corridors, Williamson Creek crossings, and elevated greens protected by traditional bunkering. Play both in one day and you've had a full Texas golf education for under $80 total.

Austin Muni Legacy
GolfATX · Lions · Morris Williams
Roy Kizer is part of GolfATX, the City of Austin's municipal golf system that also includes Lions Municipal (opened 1924) and Morris Williams. The connection between Kizer the course and Kizer the man ties the whole system together — he maintained Lions Muny for 36 years, and the course named after him carries that same spirit of accessible, well-maintained public golf. Lions made history as one of the first desegregated public courses in the South. That legacy runs through the whole system.

The Deal
$35 weekday · $44 weekend
Green fees at Kizer run $35 Monday through Thursday, $41 on Friday, and $44 on weekends and holidays. Evening rates drop to $29–$34, and juniors play for $20. Carts are $18 per person. The facility includes a driving range, sand trap, chipping and putting greens, and the Joe Balander Learning Center ($5 adults, $3 juniors). The Stag & Finch snack bar keeps you fueled. Credit and debit only — no cash.

Why You Should Play It
Southeast Austin's best-kept secret
Roy Kizer is southeast Austin's best-kept secret — a links-style muni with wildlife, wind, and more than 40 bunkers, built on land that used to process sewage. The green fees are friendly, the pace is usually manageable, and the diversity of the player base reflects Austin itself. It's not the prettiest course in the city, but it might be the most interesting. Pair it with Jimmy Clay next door and you've got one of the best 36-hole muni days in Texas.
