Date Night After 36 — Where to Take Them When You Played All Weekend

15 Spots·Austin, TX · Verified Aug 2026

Here is the math of a 36-hole weekend: ten hours on the course, two more at the range or the bar, and a partner at home who has watched you leave in the dark twice. Dinner stops being a suggestion at that point. Dinner is the peace treaty, and Austin has an unusually good set of rooms to sign it in.

The rooms run at three volumes. The Splurge is for real occasions and real apologies: the hearth at Hestia, the hardest sushi table at Uchiko, and the 1950s laundromat on Holly Street that Launderette turned into one of Austin’s defining restaurants. The Sweet Spot is special without ceremony, from a gravel garden patio in Clarksville to a 1937 bungalow that seats late and pours later. The Casual Close covers the nights the second eighteen took everything you had: wine bars, brisket, tacos and fifty-year-old queso.

Austin restaurants turn over like Austin tee sheets. Olamaie closed in July after twelve years and a Michelin star, and the original Bufalina went dark on Cesar Chavez, both inside the same summer. Everything here was open in late August 2026. And yes, there is a counter-service wine bar in town called Birdie’s, Food & Wine named it Restaurant of the Year in 2023, and it is spectacular.

The splurge tier takes reservations the way a Saturday morning tee time does, and Uchiko wants weeks of notice. Reserve dinner the same day you reserve the golf, and the whole weekend becomes defensible.

The Splurge

Six spots · $$$ to $$$$The full-apology tier runs from a Michelin star to the hardest sushi table in town, by way of red-sauce glamour and the converted laundromat that started the idea. Book these when you book the tee times, not after.

1/3
East Austin (Holly) · $$$
Launderette
Launderette took over a 1950s laundromat on Holly Street in 2015 and has been a Michelin Guide listing and a neighborhood room ever since, with a new head chef in the kitchen since December. Order the plancha burger and the wood-grilled octopus, then finish with the birthday cake ice cream sandwich that Eater counts among Austin’s most iconic dishes. The neon sign out front does a lot of the work.

1/4
Downtown (W 3rd) · $$$$
Hestia
Kevin Fink cooks over live fire at Hestia, and the room held a Michelin star in both 2024 and 2025. The scallops come off the hearth and the sourdough arrives with beef-fat butter, because everything here passes through the flame first. If the weekend included a personal best, this is the room where you tell the story.

1/3
Rosedale (Burnet) · $$$$
Uchiko
Uchiko is Tyson Cole’s flagship sibling and the hardest table of the six, so book weeks out. Ham and eggs — crispy pork belly under a soft yolk — and the jasmine cream brûlée have been settling Austin arguments for fifteen years. Reserve it the same day you reserve the weekend tee times.

1/3
West 6th · $$$
Sammie’s Italian
Sammie’s Italian is the McGuire Moorman group’s red-sauce room, which means veal parm, martinis and a valet stand out front. The dining room runs to midnight on weekends. It is the right answer when the second eighteen finished at seven and the evening still needs to feel like an occasion.

1/2
East Austin · $$$
Este
Este comes from the Suerte team and cooks coastal Mexican seafood — aguachiles, wood-fired whole fish — out of the prettiest blue building on the east side. The reservation is the hard part. The hour on the patio once you have it is the easiest thing about the whole weekend.

1/4
Old West Austin · $$$
Clark’s Oyster Bar
Clark’s is a tiny preppy-nautical room on West 6th that has been the city’s default celebration spot since 2012. Order oysters, a lobster roll and something cold and French by the glass. The room is small enough that the reservation is the hard part, though the bar seats reward walk-in optimism.

The Sweet Spot

Five spots · $$ to $$$These rooms are special without the ceremony: garden patios, candlelit brasseries, a Caribbean room on a global best-of list and the best comeback story in Austin dining. Most of them can absorb a same-day plan.

1/3
Clarksville · $$$
Josephine House
Josephine House sits in a blue cottage in Clarksville with a gravel garden patio, next door to its sister restaurant Jeffrey’s. This is the golden-hour option, so go before sunset and take a table outside. The steak frites and the string lights will do the apologizing for a five-hour absence.

1/4
South Lamar · $$$
Odd Duck
Odd Duck is Bryce Gilmore’s farm-to-table original and a Michelin Bib Gourmand, and it started life as a trailer before it turned into an institution. The small plates are the best ordering in town. The move is to hand the menu back and ask the kitchen to feed you.

1/2
East 5th · $$$
Justine’s Brasserie
Justine’s runs out of a 1937 bungalow in candlelight, seats late and pours later. Steak frites, escargot and a bottle of red arrive with no rush attached to any of it. When the second round ran long and every other kitchen in town is closing, Justine’s is the insurance policy.

1/3
East Austin · $$$
Canje
Canje is Tavel Bristol-Joseph’s modern Caribbean room, where pepper fish and West Indian curry come out of the kitchen and the rum drinks come off the bar. It is loud, warm and fully alive. Food & Wine ranked it No. 3 on the 2026 Global Tastemakers list.

1/2
West Campus · $$
Texas French Bread
A fire took the 1981 institution down in January 2022, and Texas French Bread reopened on Rio Grande this spring, four and a half years later. Dinner service runs Tuesday through Saturday, farm-to-table, with lamb meatballs and trout with braised cabbage on the menu. Bring someone who understands why that matters.

The Casual Close

Four spots · all $$The second eighteen takes something out of you, and these four ask for nothing back: walk-in wine bars, smoked brisket with a patio, Beard-winning tacos and fifty-year-old queso. One of them is named Birdie’s.

1/2
East 12th · $$
Birdie’s
Birdie’s is a counter-service wine bar whose name settles where you go after a round with birdies in it. Food & Wine named it Restaurant of the Year in 2023, and both halves of the couple who run it hold Beard nominations. Seasonal pastas and an olive-oil soft-serve sundae draw a line that forms before the doors open.

1/3
South Lamar · $$
Loro
Loro is Aaron Franklin and Tyson Cole’s Asian smokehouse, which means smoked brisket with chili gastrique, oak-grilled ribs and frozen drinks under a big shaded patio. Nobody takes a reservation and nobody minds sandals. It works when date night has to start twenty minutes after the last putt.

1/3
East 12th · $$
Nixta Taqueria
Edgar Rico runs an heirloom-masa taqueria with a James Beard Best Emerging Chef award and a TIME100 nod behind it, and the cooking carries both: duck carnitas tacos, a beet “tartare” tostada. Nixta is casual in its price and its dress code and nowhere else.

South Lamar · $$
Matt’s El Rancho
Matt’s El Rancho has been serving Tex-Mex since 1952, and it is the answer for the partner who wants a Bob Armstrong queso and a frozen margarita rather than a tasting menu and a recap of your back nine. The place closes on Tuesdays and there will be a wait, and the wait is part of the arrangement.

What is the best date night restaurant in East Austin?

Launderette — the converted 1950s laundromat on Holly Street that has anchored east-side dining since 2015. It's Michelin Guide listed, the patio is one of the neighborhood's best, and the birthday cake ice cream sandwich is on Eater's list of Austin's most iconic dishes. Este, Canje and Justine's Brasserie give the east side three more at different volumes.

Where should we eat after playing golf in Austin?

Depends on the state of you. Straight off the course: Loro's patio or Matt's El Rancho, no shower judgment. Cleaned up: Launderette or Josephine House at golden hour. Celebrating a career round: Hestia, which held a Michelin star in 2024 and 2025.

Which Austin restaurants are hardest to book?

From this list: Uchiko (book weeks out), Este, Suerte's sibling reservations generally, Clark's (a very small room) and Sammie's on weekends. Birdie's takes no reservations at all — the line forms before open, which is its own kind of date.

Is Birdie's in Austin related to golf?

No — it's a counter-service wine bar on East 12th named Birdie's, run by Tracy Malechek-Ezekiel and Arjav Ezekiel. But it was Food & Wine's 2023 Restaurant of the Year, both owners have James Beard nominations, and we are constitutionally incapable of leaving a place called Birdie's out of a golf publication's dinner list.

Did Texas French Bread reopen?

Yes. The 1981 bakery-turned-bistro was destroyed by fire in January 2022 and reopened in spring 2026 at 2900 Rio Grande in West Campus, with dinner service Tuesday through Saturday — lamb meatballs, trout with braised cabbage, and the bread that made the name.

What happened to Olamaie?

It closed in July 2026 after twelve years, Michelin star and all — which is why it isn't on this list. Maie Day, Michael Fojtasek's chophouse at the South Congress Hotel, carries the lineage if you were an Olamaie loyalist.

What are the most romantic restaurants in Austin?

Candlelight and patios do the work: Justine's Brasserie (a 1937 bungalow, late seatings), Josephine House's garden in Clarksville, Este's courtyard at dusk, and Clark's if your idea of romance involves oysters in a room the size of a rail car.

Where can you get a late dinner in Austin?

Justine's Brasserie is the classic late-night answer — seating well past most kitchens' close since 2009. Sammie's runs to midnight on weekends. Both are on this list precisely because 36 holes has a way of destroying a dinner schedule.

What is the dress code situation at these spots?

The casual tier (Birdie's, Loro, Nixta, Matt's) is come-as-you-are. The sweet spot is clean-shirt territory. For Hestia, Uchiko and Sammie's, change out of the golf clothes — you own a Players Shirt for exactly this occasion.

How were these restaurants chosen?

Every spot was verified open as of late August 2026 — which mattered, because the research caught two significant closures this summer. The list optimizes for one scenario: you spent the weekend on a golf course, you owe someone a great dinner, and Austin has fifteen correct answers at three volumes.
