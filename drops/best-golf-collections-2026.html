Our Favorite Golf Collections of 2026

Drops & Brands · Year in Review 12 collections · 48 pieces · prices checked November 15, 2026
The TGI Take

We covered dozens of golf collections this year. These are the twelve we kept coming back to.

How we picked them. Design first: did the collection have a point of view, and did every piece belong to it? Then depth, because a full wardrobe beats a single great polo. Then whether it works on a course and off it. We kept to independent golf labels and fashion brands doing golf. Big golf companies only count as collab partners, which is how FootJoy, PUMA, Callaway, Vessel and Red Wing got in.

What is still out there. Each entry has four standout pieces with prices we checked on November 15. Plenty of spring pieces have sold out; we kept them in with a sold-out tag rather than pretend the collection was something else. ALD’s capsule is gone entirely, and Gumtree × PUMA now lives at PUMA Europe at outlet prices. Each entry links to our original coverage.

What 2026 looked like. Fashion brands stopped dabbling. ALD put out its third and biggest golf capsule, OVO went from equipment to a full clothing line, and A.P.C. teamed up with Tokyo’s Cph/Golf. The independents moved past the polo: Metalwood’s fall collection has seven pieces of outerwear and one polo, Malbon built its season around workwear denim, and Manors went deep on merino and insulation. The knit polo became the shirt of fall. And some of the best work came from well outside the usual places, like Australia’s Walker and Cape Town’s Odd Ritual.

The verdict. Metalwood Studio FW26 is our collection of the year: the most complete, the most confident, and the clearest look at where golf clothing is heading. It comes first below, with Malbon’s Ironworks right behind it.

“Golf is a contact sport. You’re just not playing it correctly.”— Metalwood Studio, on the Goalie Jersey
Los Angeles · 36 styles

Metalwood Studio, FW26

When we profiled Metalwood in June, it ran on medieval heraldry and ’90s tour polos. FW26 shows founder Cole Young building something bigger than a golf brand: of 36 styles, one is a polo and seven are outerwear. There’s a padded goalie jersey with a 94 on the back, a reversible tee, and western yokes on the track jacket and the blouson.

Golf still shows up in the graphics, like the iGolf hat, but the clothes are cut for the rest of the week, and the fits keep getting bigger. It’s a fashion collection with a golf accent rather than golf clothing with a fashion edge, and it’s the clearest picture of where independent golf clothing went this year. The “Pt 1” on the lookbook says more is coming. All four pieces below are in stock.

“The Foreman Jacket reimagines classic workwear through the Malbon lens.”— Malbon Golf
Los Angeles · Fall 2026

Malbon Golf, The Ironworks

Malbon’s fall collection runs to 88 pieces, and about 27 of them matter. What holds them together is Ironworks Division, built from heavyweight stone-washed hickory-stripe denim, the striped cotton painters and railroad crews wore, and run across a jacket, a carpenter short, overalls, a sweatshirt, a snapback and a driver headcover. It’s the least golf-looking thing Malbon has made.

Under that run a herringbone twill called Station, an all-over Inkwash print on two windshirts and a short, and two knits doing the quiet work logos usually do. Two collaborations close it out, and both are better than anything Malbon made on its own: a Red Wing Shop Moc oxford and a CLUCT divot tool made in Japan.

“It’s one of the only sports where you can actually choose what to look like and how to dress.”— Jojo Regan, Manors co-founder
London · 22 pieces

Manors Golf, AW26 Honourable Company

The Honourable Company is 22 pieces built around merino, PrimaLoft insulation and Cordura ripstop. The name points at the Honourable Company of Edinburgh Golfers, the club that wrote the first surviving rules of golf in 1744, and Manors has always borrowed that formal vocabulary (Stableford, Greenskeeper, Ranger) and then cut the trousers wide and pleated.

It’s a layering collection, which is the right answer to a season where the first tee is cold and the ninth is not. Merino holds warmth when the temperature drops and sheds it when you walk uphill; PrimaLoft keeps insulating when it gets wet, where down would not. It’s the most technical collection on this list, and all four pieces below are in stock.

“New styles thoughtfully designed for the modern game.”— Aimé Leon Dore, on its third golf capsule
New York · Released April 23

Aimé Leon Dore, SS26 Golf

ALD’s third golf capsule landed April 23 and ran 55 pieces: Blackwatch plaid windbreakers and popovers, double-pleated Cordura pants and shorts, merino intarsia knits, diamond jacquard quarter zips and croc-embossed FootJoy Premiere Series Marquis shoes. Performance fabrics throughout, and nothing that looks like it came from a pro shop.

The FootJoys were the headline, waterproof leather with a co-branded insole, and they sold out in both colorways. The knits were the quieter win: the kind you’d wear to dinner after the round without anyone knowing they were golf clothes. Every piece below is sold out. It still set the look for the year.

Paris × Tokyo · Released September 2

A.P.C. Golf × Cph/Golf

The two marks don’t sit side by side on these 18 pieces. They were welded into one, A.P.Cph, on the chest of nearly everything A.P.C. Golf and Tokyo’s Cph/Golf released on September 2. Several tops carry 39 RUE MADAME PARIS, A.P.C.’s head office, across the back, and the half zip runs it against Cph/Golf’s own house line, NOT A GOLF GENTLEMAN.

Everything sits in black, navy, white, cream and light gray. It was a Japan-only release on a Japan-only store, and six of the eighteen pieces sold out on day one, including the jacket and the polo below. The pants and the green fork are still on Captain’s Helm’s site.

“I like to describe Gumtree to people as almost a slow-fashion golf brand.”— Karsten Jurkschat, Gumtree founder
Collab · Released April

Gumtree Golf × PUMA, Field Notes

A brand that hand-sews headcovers from vintage fishing waders, working with PUMA. Field Notes put Gumtree’s nature-first ideas into polos, tees, shorts and a cap at PUMA’s scale, and it was the indie-meets-big-brand collab of the year.

Gumtree puts two percent of every purchase toward pulling carbon out of the atmosphere, and the collection carries that through to the details, from the print along the hems of the shorts to the conservation lettering on the cap. It’s sold out on PUMA’s US site. PUMA Europe still has it, at outlet prices that make it the cheapest entry on this list.

Los Angeles · The Curriculum and Fall Drop 2

Students, Fall 2026

Students released fall in two parts. The Curriculum landed in September, led by the Denali Stadium Jacket, a heavy wool jacket that gets better with years, and double-pleated wool trousers. Fall Drop 2 followed on September 30 with two jackets in an oil-slick camo the brand says is based on a golf course pond, plus waffle-knit and diamond-knit sweater polos.

Together they make the fullest fall wardrobe any streetwear-golf label put out this year, and they hit every idea of the season: the knit polo as the shirt of fall, texture over shiny polyester, and a real jacket for the walk to the first tee. Every piece below is in stock.

“All I’ve ever wanted to do is make cool stuff with my friends.”— Ian Gilley, Sugarloaf Social Club founder
McLean, Virginia · Released August

Sugarloaf Social Club, The Hidden Gem Collection

Hidden Gem is a very Sugarloaf Social Club list: an $850 MacKenzie bag next to a $16 frisbee. The McLean, Virginia brand calls itself a beacon for golf nerds who like a mix of high and low, and this collection is that idea turned into product, with USA-made wool headcovers and a Portland-built leather bag at one end and a Nalgene and a Crazy Creek chair at the other.

It reads less like a golf collection than like packing for a weekend where golf happens to be involved, which is exactly why it made the list. Three pieces sold out on day one, and the MacKenzie and the Nalgene are gone now too. The windshirt and the chair are still there.

Toronto · Apparel, Callaway and Vessel

OVO Golf, The 2026 Collection

OVO Golf launched in July 2025 with a Callaway and Vessel equipment capsule that sold out within hours. 2026 is where it stopped being a capsule and became a brand: stretch nylon polos, vests and chinos in Midnight Green, Deep Denim and Sandstone, basketweave sweater vests that nod to old country club knits, and open-knit sweater polos with contrast tipping.

A second equipment drop in July added an Opus SP wedge, Chrome Tour Triple Diamond balls and the Monogram Lux stand bag, and most of it sold out inside 72 hours. The knits and the bag are gone. The nylon pieces, in four-way stretch that is water-repellent and UV-cut, are still in.

Public Drip, FW26 Nightshift

Public Drip called its fall collection Nightshift, and the name does the describing: coffee, cream, charcoal, black, navy and raw indigo, cut for the round that starts after work and ends somewhere that isn’t a golf course.

The Brooklyn label built its name making New York muni golf look like a scene, and this is its most tailored outing yet: a waffle-knit long-sleeve polo that reads as a sweater, a herringbone half zip with a gold YKK pull, and pleated trousers that would pass at a downtown office. Everything below is under $175, and all of it is still in stock at full price.

“A Walker Golf Things ode to the course itself.”— Walker Golf Things, on Blooming Grounds
Australia · Released August 21

Walker Golf Things, Blooming Grounds

Walker Golf Things draw everything by hand, and Blooming Grounds is the fullest expression of that they have put out. The Australian brand released it on August 21 as an ode to spring and the flora along the fairways, which is exactly right in Melbourne and a forward buy in an Austin August.

One piece matters more than the rest. The knit polo has a whole landscape knitted into the body as a jacquard, so the picture is made from the yarn itself rather than printed on top. The same scene appears as a print on the tee, and seeing the two side by side makes the point better than any description. The polo sold out; the tee, the hood and the cap are now marked down.

“Our flagship product is the polo, and we call it the most versatile polo on the planet.”— Dom Natalizio, Mantra co-founder
San Francisco · Released October 8

Mantra Golf, The Sonoran Desert Collection

Every season Mantra builds a collection around a landscape, from the Scottish Highlands and the Norwegian fjords to the redwoods. This fall it went to the Sonoran Desert, shot in Arizona in late September, and it’s the best collection the San Francisco brand has made, mostly because of the Legacy fleece, its first heavyweight layer.

The colors are clay, prickly pear, agave and sand. The standout is a cream Legacy crewneck covered in hand-drawn embroidery of North American birds, and there’s a long-sleeve pique polo in a checkerboard straight out of a 1970s pro shop. Mantra is Climate Neutral certified and gives at least 1% of sales to conservation. Everything below is in stock, and the quarter zip is on sale.
