Best Golf Magazines That Caught Our Eye This Year

24 Titles Majors & Independents Updated August 2026

Golf publishing doesn’t start and end at the Big Three. Somewhere between the corporate titles and the algorithm, a quiet scene has been growing — quarterlies with zero ads, biannuals printed in Melbourne, European titles covering golf through ecology and politics, Substacks going deeper on course architecture than anything on a newsstand.

These are the ones that caught our eye this year. Nineteen independent titles across three tiers — premium quarterlies that treat every issue like a coffee-table book, indie print from the UK, Europe, Australia and the US, and digital-first outlets doing the best long-form writing online — plus five newsletters and deep cuts we read every week. If it runs equipment roundups or swing-tip listicles as its bread and butter, it didn’t make the cut.

Twenty-five titles, nine countries, everything from free to about $100 a year. Updated August 2026.

The Independents — 20 Titles, 3 Tiers

Premium Quarterlies
The Golfer’s Journal
Quarterly · ~$100/yr
124 pages a quarter, zero ads — a rule the magazine has never broken. Long-form storytelling, course photography that gets printed like it matters, and cultural essays that assume you can read more than 400 words at a stretch. Every issue is built to live on a shelf, not in a recycling bin, and after a few of them the subscription stops feeling expensive. This is the gold standard for independent golf publishing, and everything else on this list exists in the space it opened.
Visit The Golfer’s Journal ↗
LINKS Magazine
Quarterly · ~$26/yr
Running out of Hilton Head since 1988, which makes it the elder statesman of this list. LINKS covers the intersection of golf travel, course architecture, and lifestyle for the destination golfer — the reader planning a Bandon trip, not grinding a handicap. At about $26 a year it is also the cheapest print subscription here, and the photography alone covers the ticket.
Visit LINKS Magazine ↗
Kingdom Magazine
Quarterly · 200+ Pages
Co-founded with Arnold Palmer, which tells you the register it operates in. Kingdom runs past 200 pages an issue — luxury travel, course design, gear, and culture, produced with the heft of a fashion quarterly. It is the magazine for the golfer whose reading happens on planes to places with caddies. Not subtle, but it never claimed to be.
Visit Kingdom ↗
Catalogue 18
Annual · 300 Pages
Golf’s first subscription art magazine, and still the only one really trying it: 300 pages a year of course photography treated as fine art, printed once annually and built to be collected rather than consumed. There is no news in it and nothing dates. If the coffee-table book is your favorite golf format, this is the standing order to place.
Visit Catalogue 18 ↗
Golf Course Architecture
Quarterly · Global
The global journal of golf design and development, published quarterly by Tudor Rose. New builds, renovations, routing philosophy, turf, and the business behind all of it — written seriously enough that the architects themselves read it. If The Fried Egg is the fan’s architecture outlet, this is the trade paper, and eavesdropping on the trade is half the fun.
Visit Golf Course Architecture ↗
Bunkered
10x/Year · Glasgow
Scotland’s biggest golf title, running out of Glasgow since 1996 at ten issues a year. Bunkered is lively and opinionated in a way the glossy monthlies rarely risk — it will pick a fight on an equipment ruling and put a scowling tour pro on the cover. It covers the game from a distinctly Scottish perspective, which means the golf itself is taken seriously and everything else slightly less so.
Visit Bunkered ↗
Indie Coffee Table & Culture
Depeche Golf
From Europe · €22/issue
“From Europe with love.” Depeche treats golf as a lens on the world rather than a sport in a vacuum — culture, arts, ecology, and politics move through every issue. Five issues deep and getting better each time, and the fifth came bundled with a special-edition Mogshade headcover, which tells you the company it keeps. The most intellectually ambitious title on this list.
Visit Depeche Golf ↗
Fairways
France · La Culture Golf
“Le magazine de la culture golf.” The French entry on this list, covering the game the way the French cover everything — travel, style, courses, and tour golf treated as culture rather than content. Club-house essays, best-golfs rankings, and its own fairways Cup event. Proof the indie golf-culture wave speaks more languages than English.
Visit Fairways ↗
GolfPunk
Germany · The Cult Revival
The German revival of the cult UK title that made golf publishing irreverent in the 2000s. GolfPunk’s promise is that a golf magazine can be funny, loose, and competent at the same time — comic-book cover art, travel features, and gear coverage that refuses to take itself seriously. The most fun anyone is having in European golf media.
Visit GolfPunk ↗
The Links Diary
Triannual · Scotland · £17
Words, photography, and film from Scottish fairways — no swing tips, no product reviews, no exceptions. Fourteen issues in, The Links Diary has kept a quiet, literary register that reads closer to a journal entry than a magazine feature, published three times a year at £17. It is the title on this list most likely to make you book a ferry.
Visit The Links Diary ↗
Golf Out
UK Indie · £18/issue
Seven issues in, and built almost entirely on photography and feel — the kind of UK indie where the layout is the argument. Golf Out looks as good on a shelf as it reads on a couch, and it is sold through Soft Hands Club, which is exactly the right shop window for it. £18 an issue, and issues do not hang around.
Shop at Soft Hands Club ↗
McKellar Magazine
A Golf Companion · ~$14/issue
Eighty pages per issue by an ex-Guardian golf correspondent and an architecture writer, self-described as “A Golf Companion” — which is exactly the right phrase. McKellar mixes writing, illustration, and course profiles with a literary sensibility that no other golf title matches. Around $14 an issue, and the closest thing golf has to a lit journal.
Visit McKellar ↗
Caddie Magazine
Biannual · Melbourne, Australia
Founded in Melbourne by William Watt, and published twice a year with a remit no one else touches: golf history, sociology, and stories from Arctic courses to ancient Philippine driving ranges. No tour coverage, no gear reviews, no swing tips — just the parts of golf that are actually interesting to read about ten years later. The indie treatment, done right.
Read about Caddie ↗
Soft Hands Club
Journal · UK
The editorial arm of the UK’s best indie golf retailer, and proof a shop journal can be a real publication. Soft Hands covers the territory where skate, surf, art, and design culture meet the course — interviews, essays, and product writing with actual taste. Read it the way you would a record-store zine: partly for the words, partly to know what to buy next.
Read the Journal ↗
Digital Editorial & Newsletters
The Fried Egg
Newsletter · US
The best golf architecture newsletter going, and it is not close. Deep course profiles, design history, and long-form essays arrive free in your inbox, backed by a podcast network that treats routing philosophy as a subject for adults. If you care about why a hole works rather than how far someone hit it, The Fried Egg is your primary source. Start with any course profile and lose an afternoon.
Visit The Fried Egg ↗
No Laying Up
Podcast + Editorial · US
Started as a podcast and grew into the most candid editorial voice in golf media — witty, opinionated, and unafraid to say what the broadcast will not. The written work sits alongside the pods and films now, covering tour golf and course culture with the tone of your sharpest group chat. The membership model means it answers to its audience rather than to advertisers, and it reads that way.
Visit No Laying Up ↗
The Quadrilateral
Substack · Newsletter
Geoff Shackelford territory: thoughtful golf writing on Substack, centered on the major championships and the institutions around them, with interviews and cultural commentary alongside. The Quadrilateral is the kind of quiet, considered writing that does not chase clicks — it arrives when there is something to say and assumes you would rather read one good piece than five fast ones.
Read on Substack ↗
Evalu18
Architecture Reviews · Online
Course-by-course architecture reviews plus something genuinely rare: a maintained directory of independent golf magazines, which makes Evalu18 the best single resource for finding which indie titles are still in print. We leaned on it building this list. Between the reviews and the curation it functions as a map of the whole golf-media underground.
Visit Evalu18 ↗
Moegolf
Culture Blog · Online
A golf culture blog with a collector’s eye — coffee-table book reviews, indie magazine coverage, and a curatorial sensibility that treats golf media itself as a design object. Moegolf is where you go to find out a book exists before it sells out. Niche inside niche, and better for it.
Visit Moegolf ↗
By Design (ASGCA)
Quarterly · Free Digital
The quarterly from the American Society of Golf Course Architects — course design explained by the people who actually build them. It is free, digital, and completely without hype: renovation case studies, member Q&As, and the practical mechanics of how a course gets made. The insider view, at the best price on this list.
Visit ASGCA ↗

Newsletters & Digital Deep Cuts — 4 More

Monday Q Info
Substack · Weekly · Free
Ryan French’s newsletter about the players grinding through Monday qualifiers trying to reach the PGA Tour — the guys nobody televises. It is the most human writing in golf media right now, and it has turned French into one of the sport’s most trusted reporters from a standing start.
Subscribe ↗
Cookie Jar Golf
Britain · Est. 2020
An independent British outlet started in 2020 by, in their words, a few golfing tragics. Pro tours and the amateur game, course architecture, travel, and the written word — plus one of the better golf podcasts going. The closest thing Britain has to a golf culture magazine that talks like a person.
Read ↗
Golf Club Atlas
Online · Since 1999
The home of golf course architecture on the internet since 1999, and the archive every architecture writer working today grew up reading. The course profiles and the discussion boards are a genuine education — start here before any of the newer architecture outlets.
Read ↗
Fire Pit Collective
Online · Film & Longform
A storytelling company more than a magazine — courses, travel, features, and a podcast, built on immersing in a place and finding the heart of it. When it lands, it produces the best filmed golf journalism anywhere.

Golf Magazines — Frequently Asked

What are the best golf magazines?
For writing and photography you keep on a shelf: The Golfer’s Journal, McKellar, Catalogue 18, and The Links Diary. For course architecture: The Fried Egg, Golf Club Atlas, and Golf Course Architecture. For weekly reading: Monday Q Info. These are independent titles — if you want equipment testing and instruction, the newsstand majors still do that better.
What are the best golf newsletters?
Monday Q Info for the human stories behind PGA Tour qualifying, The Fried Egg for course architecture, and The Quadrilateral for majors analysis. All three are free or close to it.
What is the oldest golf magazine?
Golf Monthly, published in London by Future plc, is the oldest golf magazine in the world. Its first editor was Harold Hilton, who won the Open Championship in 1892 and 1897.
What is the best independent golf magazine?
The Golfer’s Journal is the most established — 124 pages a quarter with no advertising. McKellar is the connoisseur’s pick, written by an ex-Guardian golf correspondent and an architecture writer. Catalogue 18 is the art object of the group at 300 pages a year.
Are golf magazine subscriptions still a good buy in 2026?
The independents cost more per issue than a newsstand title but are built to be kept rather than recycled — most run between $15 and $30 an issue. Several of the best are free: The Fried Egg, Monday Q Info, Golf Club Atlas, and By Design from the American Society of Golf Course Architects.
Which golf magazine is best for course architecture?
The Fried Egg is the best free option and publishes the deepest course profiles going. Golf Course Architecture, published quarterly by Tudor Rose, is the industry journal. By Design from the ASGCA is free and written by the architects themselves.
