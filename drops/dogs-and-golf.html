Dogs and Golf: Bring Them Along (Carefully)

By Lenny Harrington · Updated October 22, 2026

The Grassy Issue · Dogs on the course29 picks · prices checked October 22, 2026
The TGI Take

We’re all for dogs on the golf course. Not every course and not every round, but where they’re allowed, bring your dog along. A walking round with a dog at your heel is about as good as golf gets: the pace slows down, nobody minds the bogey, and you stop staring at your phone between shots.

Be careful about it. A dog on the course is a guest, and so are you. Keep your dog on a leash, off the greens and out of the bunkers, pick up after them, and keep them quiet when someone’s hitting. Start with a short loop on a quiet evening before you try eighteen together. If the club says no dogs, it’s no dogs.

The British figured this out a long time ago. On the old links of the UK and Ireland dogs are part of the scenery. The Old Course at St Andrews closes on Sundays and turns into one of the biggest dog parks in the world, and Sunningdale’s club historian says overseas visitors are surprised by how many dogs they see. In the US it’s rarer, but closer than you think: Austin’s own city courses allow leashed dogs.

The best golf dogs belong to the greenskeepers. Every good course seems to have one riding in the maintenance cart: Blu, the pit mix who chases geese for the grounds crew at Detroit Golf Club; Lucky, Lefty and Charlie, the goose dogs at Pebble Beach; Rio, the superintendent’s Lab at King’s Deer in Colorado. Golf Digest put it simply: most courses allow only one dog, the superintendent’s. Give a wave on the way past.

Etiquette

How to Bring a Dog Golfing

  1. Ask first. Check the course policy, and if you’re a guest, ask your host or the pro shop.
  2. Leash on. “On a leash and under control” is the standard almost everywhere.
  3. Off the greens, tees and bunkers. Paw prints in a bunker are your job to rake.
  4. Carry bags, and use them. No exceptions.
  5. Quiet on the shot. The best golf dogs sit when someone’s swinging.
  6. Walk, don’t ride. A dog tied up in a moving cart is a bad idea.
  7. Start small. Trainer Jonathan Klein’s advice is to treat the first two or three rounds as training.
  8. Bring water. Especially in Texas.
“Everyone knows a tired dog is a good dog, and we get a lot of good dogs here.”— Mike Beaver, Indian Valley Golf Club, to Golf Digest

Where to Go

Where You Can Bring a Dog

Austin. The City of Austin’s rules for Roy Kizer say it plainly: “Dogs must be on a leash and under control.” Golf Advisor reported in 2015 that every Austin muni allowed dogs, along with Delaware Springs in Burnet (“scoop the poop, and keep them on a leash”). Policies change, so call the pro shop before you load your dog in the car.

Elsewhere in the US. Golf Digest has named Belgrade Lakes in Maine, Indian Valley in Marin County and Seaside in Oregon as dog-friendly. Plenty of others allow it quietly or with permission, and plenty don’t at all. Ask first, every time.

The UK and Ireland. Golf Digest says dogs are more likely to be welcome at old links with common walking ground than at newer inland clubs. The St Andrews Links are a public park with dogs on them every day, Cruden Bay is known for it, and Scotland’s Outdoor Access Code asks walkers crossing a course to keep dogs on a short lead.

The Questions

Can you bring a dog on a golf course?

Some courses allow it, usually on a leash, off the greens and tees, and with the owner cleaning up. Always check the course policy first. Many UK and Irish links courses welcome dogs; in the US it is rarer.

Are dogs allowed on Austin golf courses?

The City of Austin's rules for Roy Kizer Golf Course say dogs must be on a leash and under control. Check with each course's pro shop before you go, as policies can change.

Can you take a dog on the Old Course at St Andrews?

The Old Course closes to golf on Sundays and the public, dogs included, can walk it. Pick up after your dog and stay off the greens.

What should a dog wear on the golf course?

A secure collar and a leash, plus a bandana or cooling layer in heat. Bring water and a bowl, and waste bags.
