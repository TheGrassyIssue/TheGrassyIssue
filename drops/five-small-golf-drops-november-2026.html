Five Small Drops, November 2026

The Edit · New drops 21 picks from 5 drops · checked November 19, 2026
The TGI Take

November is when the big brands go loud: holiday collections, bundles, a hundred new products at a time. The drops we like best this month went the other way. Five small brands and collaborations put out a handful of pieces each, every one of them built around a single idea you can explain in a sentence.

How we found them. We checked the stores of 133 brands in our index for everything added since mid-October, then looked further afield for debuts and collaborations, and set aside anything we had already covered. What was left: a Hong Kong collaboration in Japanese nylon, a one-layer rain suit, a Stockholm anorak built to be worn over its own midlayer, a family label making knitwear in Italy and two jackets from brands founded 44 years apart.

What ties them together. Material and origin. MacLowly nylon and hand-loomed Portuguese wool, Swiss Schoeller made up in New York, three-layer Swedish rain gear, Piedmont merino and Giza cotton finished in Emilia-Romagna, wool and leather on a varsity. Each section below starts with the idea, then the lookbook, then the pieces.

What is in here. 21 pieces from five drops, none of them featured on TGI before. Prices are what each store shows a US visitor; Hendrikx sells in euros and Local Rule in kronor, so we list a rough dollar figure beside those. Everything was in stock when we checked on November 19, 2026.

Mogshade × Incense Harbour and Fabricaal

Two capsules in two weeks · Nov 4–12Water-repellent Japanese nylon with a three-person Hong Kong label, then hand-loomed Portuguese merino.

Who they made it with. Incense Harbour is a small Hong Kong label run by Erick Sze and a team of three, which its listing at the city’s PMQ design hub says began during COVID, when the founders were looking at Hong Kong with fresh eyes. The name is a direct translation of the Chinese name for Hong Kong, 香港. Mogshade describes the drop as bringing together Incense Harbour’s “distinctive approach to everyday objects” and its own love of textiles, craftsmanship and the game.

The fabric. Everything in the Incense Harbour drop is cut from MacLowly, a Japanese water-repellent nylon, and the pouches are lined in Portuguese wool. The second capsule goes the other way: Fabricaal, short for Fábrica Alentejana de Lanifícios, weaves its merino on manual wooden looms in Portugal’s Alentejo, in what Mogshade calls one of only three original secular wool handicrafts still running in Europe.

Whim: The FW26 Storm Suit

A rain suit that does not look like one · Nov 18A windbreaker and pant in Swiss Schoeller stretch nylon, made in New York, in clear water green, slate grey and apple green.

One layer, no laminate. Most rain gear is a sandwich of face fabric, membrane and lining. Whim’s Storm suit skips the sandwich: it is a single dense stretch weave of Schoeller Dynamic, a Swiss nylon, with a water-repellent finish on the outside. Wind stays out and light rain beads off, while body heat and moisture pass through. In Whim’s words, it “breathes the way a shell should, so it wears cool through a full round instead of trapping you in.”

Made to be worn as a suit. The windbreaker and pant come in matching colors and are made in New York. The lookbook leans all the way in: golfers head to toe in one color, on a grey late-fall course.

Local Rule: FW26 Tech Layers

Rain or wind, keep playing · Nov 16A three-layer waterproof anorak and a vented midlayer from Stockholm, designed to be worn one over the other.

Who they are. Local Rule is a three-person company out of Stockholm, started in 2022 by two founders from an advertising agency and named for the supplementary sheet a club prints for its own course: the sanctioned exception to the general rules. In 2025 it began moving production home to Borås. We covered the whole story in our Brand to Know.

The idea. Local Rule’s line for this drop is four words: “Rain or wind, keep playing.” The anorak is a pullover, not a jacket, so there is no open front flapping through your swing. It is cut from a three-layer waterproof, windproof fabric with a vent across the upper back to let heat out as you walk. The midlayer underneath is a nylon-elastane interlock with laser-cut ventilation. Local Rule’s own advice is to wear one over the other on cold, wet days, which is exactly how the clean Scandinavian studio lookbook shows them.

Timing. Both are pre-orders: the midlayer ships from Local Rule’s warehouse on November 25 and the anorak on December 3. Prices are in Swedish kronor.

Hendrikx: A Debut, Made in Italy

A new label from Düsseldorf · Nov 6One half-zip, one polo, two colors each and a headcover, from a family that has played golf since the 1940s.

A family thing. Hendrikx was founded in 2025 by Jan Hendrikx and his partner Karolina, working from Düsseldorf, where his family has lived since the 1850s. Golf has been in the family since the 1940s. The black-and-white photo below is Jan’s grandfather, Jochen Hendrikx, in the 1970s, and the brand says it has always loved the way he dressed and that much of what it makes still comes back to that.

Made in Italy, all the way down. The half-zip is knitted in a small family workshop in Emilia-Romagna from 19.5-micron merino spun at a 125-year-old mill in Piedmont. The polo’s collar and cuffs are knitted in Egyptian Giza cotton, and its six-hole tagua-nut buttons are sewn on by hand with thread that forms a sideways H. Even the labels and hangtags are sourced in Italy, and both pieces are machine washable. The debut campaign was shot at Château d’Augerville, south of Paris.

Eastside Golf × Avirex

Two jackets, two histories · Nov 11–12Avirex’s flight-jacket heritage meets Eastside’s golf: an MA-1 bomber reworked in nylon and a wool-and-leather varsity.

Flight jackets, meet the first tee. Avirex built its name on military flight jackets, and the MA-1 bomber is the classic shape. Eastside Golf reworks it in red nylon, puts its swinging golfer across the back and pairs it with a varsity jacket in wool and leather. “Two legacies. One statement,” is how Eastside puts it.

The details. Collaborative patches on both, an ESG crest on the bomber’s chest, a varsity E on the wool body and both brands’ founding years printed down the sleeve. It is a two-piece capsule, which is exactly the point: no tees, no hats, just the jackets.

The Last Word

Small Runs Go Fast

Two Fabricaal headcovers were gone inside a week, Local Rule’s whole autumn delivery sold through before this one, and a debut label making two shirts in two colors does not make many of either. If something here is the one, do not wait for Black Friday to find out whether it is still there.
