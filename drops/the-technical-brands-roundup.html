The Technical Brands Roundup

8 Brands · 19 Picks Fabric Is the Story Coffee Fiber, Seawool, Merino, Pima & More

Every golf brand says “performance fabric.” These eight actually mean it. No mega-brands, no pro shop staples — just independent labels that treat material science as the entire point. They’re knitting polos from recycled coffee beans. Weaving yarn from crushed oyster shells. Sourcing the longest-staple Peruvian Pima cotton on earth. Building shoes from the same CORDURA used in military packs.

We pulled 19 pieces across polos, tees, pants, shorts, outerwear, hats, and shoes. From a $32 hat to a $405 pair of Japanese-engineered walkers, every pick earns its spot by what it’s made of — not who’s wearing it.

The Polos

Cool Jade · Recycled Poly
Radmor
Sutton Yarn Dyed Stripe Knit Polo
Seattle-based Radmor uses Cool Jade — a fabric woven from recycled jade stone particles that creates a natural cooling effect on skin contact. The Sutton adds sweater-knit texture to a true performance polo. UPF 50+, moisture-wicking, biodegradable fiber.

Seawool · Oyster Shell Fiber
Manors
Course Polo
UK brand Manors weaves Seawool — a yarn made from crushed oyster shells and recycled PET bottles. The result is a fabric with natural moisture-wicking properties, odor resistance, and a hand feel softer than standard merino. Sustainable from the ocean up.
$88

Java-tek · Coffee-Infused
Reflo
Mira Polo
Reflo’s Java-tek fabric is woven from recycled coffee beans. The coffee particles speed up drying time, control odor through natural anti-bacterial properties, and provide UV protection. Single-use waste given a second life on the course. Made in Portugal.
~$95

Merino SS Golf Shirt
Pure merino wool on the golf course. Temperature-regulating, naturally antimicrobial, odor-resistant without chemical treatment. Whim sources premium fibers and lets the material do all the work — no synthetic blends, no coatings. The most expensive polo here, and it earns it.
