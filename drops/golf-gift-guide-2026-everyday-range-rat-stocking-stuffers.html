The TGI Take

This year’s gift guide is three posts and nine categories, and it is built brand by brand. A single product dropped into a category never says much on its own, so each brand here gets its own block: why it belongs in the category, and four gifts that prove it. Every brand is independent, and none appears twice across the three posts.

Part 1 covers the gifts that get used the most: everyday kit made as well as it can be, gifts for the golfer who lives at the range, and small things for a stocking. Thirty brands, about a hundred and twenty gifts, from a $6 hat clip to a sterling silver ball marker.

Where to start. For the everyday upgrade, TOTEM’s brass divot tool. For the range rat, Clutch’s Range Set. For the stocking, Birdie Balm, which puts a ball marker in the lid of a lip balm.

The Everyday Item as Well Done as Possible

Kit they already use every round, made by someone who cared

The best gift is the thing they already use every round, made better than it had to be. Nobody needs another divot tool; everybody notices when the one in their pocket is machined brass. These ten brands each make the ordinary things in a golfer’s bag (the tool, the marker, the towel, the pouch, the belt) about as well as they can be made.

The Last Word

Buy From the Brand, Not Just the Product

The reason this guide is built brand by brand is simple: the best gift is usually the second thing you buy from a brand you already trust. Find the brand that sounds like the person you are buying for, then pick the piece that fits.

Know what they play. Edel’s alignment plates only fit Edel’s Array putters, Clutch makes its sticks to order, and Magpie’s brass repair tool was down to its last two. Small brands make small runs, and stock moves fast in December.

Prices are as each store shows them to a US visitor. Sinking Birdies shows pounds, so those dollar figures are approximate. Everything was in stock on November 9, 2026. Part 2 and Part 3 cover the other six categories.
