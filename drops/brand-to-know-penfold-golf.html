Brand to Know: Penfold Golf

By Lenny Harrington, Founder & Editor · Updated November 17, 2026

Brand to Know27 picks · prices checked November 17, 2026
The TGI Take

Penfold is one of the oldest names in golf, and the one James Bond named on screen. In Goldfinger (1964), Bond swaps balls on Auric Goldfinger and wins the match on the 18th green: “Here’s my Penfold Hearts.”

The company started in 1927. A. E. Penfold had designed Dunlop’s famous Maxfli ball before leaving to set up his own factory on Bromford Lane in Birmingham, where he worked out a way to wind the rubber thread inside a ball under extreme tension. Penfold balls were marked with playing-card suits, the company opened a second factory in Brooklyn in 1935, and its About Us page says that by 1960 it was the first maker to produce more than a million balls in a year.

It was not an easy run. A. E. Penfold died in 1941 when the ship he was on was torpedoed off the coast of Ireland, and the factory was bombed during the war. His son Dick rebuilt it. Gary Player played the Penfold Ace, Manuel Piñero won the 1977 Penfold PGA Championship, and Nick Faldo won his first big title with a Penfold ball in 1978. Then the brand was sold, sold again and went quiet.

It came back through Gavin Perrett, a British expat in Pennsylvania who, Forbes reports, sent a “very cheeky” email to Penfold’s owner in 2013 and restarted the brand from his attic in January 2020. He runs Penfold Golf Limited, a licensee of the parent company in England, and he does not like the word revival: “People don’t realize that we weren’t revived—we just weren’t really doing anything.” Today Penfold makes Sunday bags in British Millerain waxed canvas, an official Ryder Cup 1927 collection, retro polos and a lot of very good ball markers. Penfold and the Ryder Cup both turn 100 in 2027.

Where to Start

For the bag: the Heritage Sunday Bag II ($289). It is the piece the rest of the line is built around, and the one most likely to be handed down.

For a gift: a sleeve of Penfold Hearts ($40) and the 007 Playing Card marker ($25). Every golfer who has seen Goldfinger will get it.

For under $50: the Heritage Headcover ($50) or the British Stripe Rugby ($50).

The Last Word

A Hundred Years Next Year

Most of the brands we cover are a few years old and still working out who they are. Penfold has the opposite problem: almost a hundred years of story, and the job of making it mean something to golfers who were not born when Bond played that ball. The waxed-canvas bags and the card-suit markers are a good answer.

Perrett told Forbes he has “three or four really big stories” planned on the way to 2027. We will be watching. Prices were checked on November 17, 2026. For more bags like these, see Upgrading Your Golf Bag.
