The TGI Take

Why Needlepoint Is Everywhere

Needlepoint has turned up in more than a dozen of our posts since the summer: a belt in our story on the clothes golf founders wanted to wear, a ball marker in a short-game kit, a flamingo headcover in The Bird Edit, a collar in a post about golf dogs. We did not plan it. It kept appearing in the stores of the brands we cover, and the best pieces kept selling out. Radry’s needlepoint blade cover and Gumtree’s State Bird headcover are both gone, and when we checked Sugarloaf Social Club this week, almost all of its fall needlepoint had sold through.

The craft is having a moment. In April, St. Louis Magazine reported that local needlepoint shops are seeing a surge led by younger stitchers. One designer, Annie Zigman, said a distributor told her sales were up 305 percent. She pointed to a 2015 industry survey that put the average needlepointer at 61 and said today’s stitchers are about 30 years younger. A shop manager there called bag tags a whole new phenomenon of the last couple of years.

Golf has always been needlepoint’s home turf. The belt with tiny clubs on it belongs to the clubhouse as much as the blazer does. For a long time you could only get one if somebody stitched it for you; Smathers & Branson started in 2004 because its founders kept getting asked where to buy the belts their girlfriends had made them, and a decade in, Austin Branson told The Daily Signal that specialty shops, colleges, yacht clubs and golf courses were still its bread and butter. What has changed is how far the makers have pushed past the belt. Smathers & Branson now stitches scorecard holders, flasks, driver covers and an official belt for the 2026 U.S. Open at Shinnecock Hills, and it made a needlepoint line with FootJoy built around FJ’s golf shoe silhouette. Asher Riley stitches driver covers and dog collars, and Brewster makes collars to match its belts. Charleston Belt put Ric Flair on one.

It fits the rest of what golfers are wearing. Prep is back in a quieter form; Who What Wear names it one of 2026’s defining moods and points to golf as one of the sports shaping the details. A needlepoint belt is the easiest way into that look, and the most personal. You pick the picture, and it is hand-stitched, slow and built to outlast the trousers you wear it with.

What we did. We went back through everything we have featured this year, checked what is still in stock and added new pieces from the same makers.

Why it works on golfers. Needlepoint is slow, flat and colorful, which suits small pictures of things you love: a course, a cart, a gopher. It is also tough. A hand-stitched canvas backed with leather will outlast the trousers you wear it with, which is why so many of these turn up in grandfathers’ drawers.

How to buy a belt. Order a size up. Bluegrass Fairway recommends two to three inches over your waist, and J.Press says one size up from your trousers. Check the width too: most are an inch and a quarter, Bluegrass Fairway’s are an inch and a half.

What is in here. There are 36 pieces from 10 makers, all in stock when we checked on November 11, 2026. Two belts and both dog collars are made to order, so allow a few weeks. Prices are what each store shows a US visitor.

The Last Word

Buy It Once

A needlepoint belt is a $125 to $200 purchase you make once. Pick a pattern that means something to you, size it up and wear it until the leather goes soft.

Then add the small stuff. A key fob or a luggage tag is the easiest way to give someone needlepoint without guessing their waist size.
