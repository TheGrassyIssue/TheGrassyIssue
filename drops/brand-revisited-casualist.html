Brand Revisited: Casualist

Brand Revisited · West London · Made in Portugal 25 picks · prices checked November 13, 2026
The TGI Take

We wrote about Casualist in May: the West London label that started life in Melbourne as Casual Pro, makes most of its clothes in Portugal and still sells the old snapback with the old name on it. Six months on, the range has not grown much, but the way it is sold has.

The kits. On November 11, Casualist put its clothes together into three outfits and priced them under the pieces bought separately: The Full Round (polo, pleated trousers and cap) at $345, The Clubhouse Kit (cardigan vest and tee) at $297 and The Weekend Kit (polo and cap) at $161. They are the brand’s Black Friday offer, and the shop is also taking 10% off with the code CASUAL10.

What else is new. The pleated trousers, the piece we have recommended most, now come in beige as well as taupe. In October, Casualist and Mogshade made wool headcovers from leftover Portuguese wool, and they sold out. The fall campaign, called Round 1, was shot by the English seaside, and it is where most of the photos below come from.

The Idea

What Casualist Is Going For

Casualist’s own account of how it started is short: there was golf, there were good looks, a few pints, a lot of laughter, and a sense that nothing on the market fit the way its people wanted to play. It names who it is not for, “not the serious players, not the performance crowd,” and who it is for: the golfer who cares how he looks without caring too much about the scorecard, and who shows up in a proper cardigan on a drizzly Tuesday morning and means it.

The line the brand keeps coming back to is that elegance and irreverence are not opposites. You can see both in the clothes. The elegance is in the construction: organic cotton pique, 100% merino, pleated trousers with a high waist, most of it made in Portugal and chosen, the brand says, for how it feels after a full round rather than how it photographs. The irreverence is in the words: ALL THE GEAR, NO IDEA across the back of a sweatshirt, a tee called Good Enough, product copy promising a uniform for 18 holes “minus the ability to actually play them well.”

Before anything is made, the team asks one question: would we wear this properly and repeatedly, not once and not for the photos? That is why the range is small, fewer than 30 products, and why so little of it changes from season to season. The kits are the clearest version of the idea: the same few pieces, worn together, every week.

Where it sits. Among the small British labels we follow, Manors is the heritage one, Sounder the technical one, Fyfe the Scottish accessory maker. Casualist is the one with the softest collar and the driest joke. It is closer in spirit to an American brand like Radmor or Sugarloaf Social Club than to anything with a crest, which makes sense for a label that started in Melbourne.

The Story

From Casual Pro to Casualist

Melbourne. The brand started in Australia as Casual Pro. The original snapback still says so: CASUAL PRO, SINCE 2023, LEISURE APPAREL, MELB – LDN – LA. The idea from the start was a label built on soft collars and jokes, for golfers who did not want to dress like a tour pro.

London, 2024. On April 2, 2024, the Casual Pro team announced the brand was moving “from the sunny shores of Melbourne, Australia, to the historic streets of London, UK.” They were open about the bet: Australian and American golf is relaxed about dress codes, and in Britain “tradition still holds sway, and golfers tend to stick to more formal attire.” A brand built on humor was moving to the strictest dress codes in the game, and its answer was to keep the jokes and add the things that get you through the clubhouse door: collars, merino, pleats.

Casualist. The rename came after the move. The founder, Elie Reboul, explains it on the about page in two sentences: the spirit was right, but the name needed to grow up a little, and Casualist is the next step, with sharper details, better quality and a clearer point of view. He kept the old snapback on sale anyway, and it is still the best seller. The navy coach jacket with CASUAL PRO across the back is still listed, under Archive.

Now. Everything is designed in West London and most of it is made in Portugal. This fall brought the Round 1 campaign by the sea, a headcover collab with Mogshade made from leftover Portuguese wool, a second color of the pleated trouser and, for Black Friday, the three kits. Elie signs off the about page with a welcome to “the enthusiasts,” the ones who play for the joy of it and know that nobody really needs to know your handicap.
