The Austin Recharge

By Lenny Harrington · Updated November 22, 2026

Mineral springs, quiet trails and a solo morning round, for the weeks after the holidays.

Field Note 17 places · checked November 22, 2026
The TGI Take

The holidays end and January starts and most of us need about a week of nothing before we are any use to anyone. This is a guide to doing that nothing well, without leaving Central Texas.

It isn’t a schedule. It is eight ideas about what a reset actually needs, with the places in and around Austin that do each one best: hot mineral water an hour south, saunas and cold plunges in town, winter trails, and a round of golf on a weekday morning with nobody waiting behind you.

Every price, hour and rule below comes from the place’s own site, or the city or state parks page, read on November 22, 2026. Things change in winter, so check before you drive.

Part I · 3 places

Go When Everyone Else Is at Work

Photo: Todd Dwyer / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

The whole thing works because it is a Tuesday. The places that are packed on a Saturday in spring are close to empty on a weekday morning in January: the first tee is yours, the parks don’t need a reservation, and Barton Springs opens at five. Take the day off, and take it in the middle of the week.

01

Barton Springs Pool

Zilker Park

Free until March 13, 2027 · 5am–10pm, swim at your own risk 5–8am · closed Thursdays 9am–7pm · 68–70°F

Three acres of spring water that holds at 68 to 70 degrees all year, which makes it the city’s own cold plunge. In January the 5am crowd is a handful of lap swimmers and steam coming off the water. It is free until mid-March, so there is no reason not to.

Official site ↗
02

Lions Municipal

Old West Austin

$35 Monday–Thursday (walking; cart $18) · book online 7 days ahead from 9am · closed the 3rd Monday

Austin’s first public course, opened in 1924, and by the city’s own account one of the first in the South to desegregate. Take the first tee time you can get on a Tuesday and carry your bag. Read our Lions story and the hole guide before you go.

Official site ↗
03

Wild Basin Wilderness Preserve

West Lake Hills

Free on weekdays, no reservation · weekends $5 with a reserved 3-hour pass · about 3 miles of trail

Austin’s first nature preserve, 50 years old this year. On a weekday you just drive in; the gate closes when the lot fills. No dogs, no bikes, no picnics, which is most of the point.

Official site ↗
Part II · 3 places

Heat, Cold, Stillness

Photo: Ottine Mineral Springs

The contrast routine is simple. Get hot in a sauna, get cold in a plunge, sit still for a while, and repeat. Austin finally has the places to do it properly, and an hour and a quarter south there is a spring that has been doing it for over a century.

04

Ottine Mineral Springs

Gonzales · about 1 hr 15 min

Tue–Thu $65 all day · Fri $75 · Sat–Sun $75 half day, $125 all day · Thu & Fri sunset soak $35 · closed Mondays · reservations only · 13+

The reason to make the drive. In 1909 oil wildcatters hit an artesian spring here; the water later fed the Gonzales Warm Springs polio hospital, which the site calls “the only desegregated place for polio care in the country at the time.” Now it is mineral pools around 102 degrees, a hot and cold plunge, a barrel sauna, hammocks and firepits. Go on a Wednesday: they call it Whisper Wednesday and keep it quiet on purpose.

Official site ↗
05

Mantle Thermal Haus

East Austin

Single sessions bookable online · memberships from $100 a month for four hours · Mon 7–11am, Tue–Sat 9am–7pm, Sun 10am–5pm

Two cedar barrel saunas, three cold plunges and a red-light lounge on Curtis Avenue. Small enough to feel like a secret. Their line: “discomfort is a pathway.”

Official site ↗
06

Ceremony Social Spa

South Lamar

Walk-ins and same-day bookings · weekdays 10am–11pm, weekends 8am–11pm · swimsuits required

“Austin’s Original BathHouse,” with two cedar saunas, indoor and outdoor cold plunges and a tea lounge. The easiest one to say yes to on the way home.

Official site ↗
Part III · 2 places

Leave the Phone in the Car

Photo: Corey Leopold / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0

The hardest part of a reset is the thing in your pocket. Pick places that make the decision for you: a bathhouse garden where devices aren’t allowed, a sauna with a no-phone rule, a canyon thirty miles out where you won’t be checking anything.

07

Bathe

East Austin

Soak passes from $29 · 8am–10pm daily · 18+ · free parking

Saunas, steam, cold plunges and thermal soaking pools, and a Soaking Garden where devices are not allowed. Bathe’s own line sums up the whole post: “HEAT. COLD. WATER. STILLNESS.” Sauna House on North Loop runs the same rule, phone-free, with wood-burning saunas.

Official site ↗
08

Pedernales Falls State Park

Johnson City · 30 miles west

$6 (13 and up) · reserve a day pass; the park often fills and turns away cars

Twin Falls Nature Trail is half a mile to the overlook; Wolf Mountain is six miles if you want to be tired. TPWD calls it “a great place to relax and recharge,” which is close enough to the brief.

Official site ↗
Part IV · 4 places

Walk Everything

Photo: Larry D. Moore / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0

No cart, no hurry. A walking round is a different round: you see the course, you talk to nobody, and the score matters less by the ninth hole. Hancock doesn’t even offer carts, and we have made the case for the slow round before. The trails are the long version of the same idea.

09

Hancock Golf Course

Hyde Park

$20 any day · 9 holes, par 35 · walking only · no tee times, first come first served · closed January 14

The oldest course in Texas and the purest solo round in town: no pro shop, a pay station, and you play when you get there. Bring a carry bag and nothing else. More in our Hancock story and the hole guide for the first.

Official site ↗
10

Roy Kizer

Southeast Austin

$35 Monday–Thursday · cart $18 · closed the 4th Monday

Open, flat and windy, and quiet on a weekday morning. A walk you can switch off on. See our Kizer hole guide for the par 3 that ruins cards.

Official site ↗
11

Barton Creek Greenbelt

South Austin

Free · 7.5-mile main trail · the 360 trailhead has a free lot and restrooms

As long or as short as you want, no reservation, ten minutes from downtown. Start at the 360 trailhead, turn around when you feel like it, and finish with Barton Springs.

Official site ↗
12

Turkey Creek Trail

Emma Long Metropolitan Park

$5 a car Mon–Thu, $10 Fri–Sun (buy online ahead on weekends) · no water or fountains until about January 15

Creek crossings and shade on the far west side. Weekdays are the move, and bring your own water this winter.

Official site ↗
Part V · 3 places

Somewhere Time Slows

Photo: Jmbuytaert / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Some places reset your clock just by being old and quiet: a garden of peacocks in Tarrytown, a bald cypress in southeast Austin, a pink granite dome above Fredericksburg.

13

Mayfield Park

Tarrytown

Free · 5am–10pm daily · 23 acres

Ponds, walled gardens and peacocks. The city’s page says it best: “When you enter Mayfield time slows.” A thirty-minute walk, not a hike, and the right way to end an afternoon.

Official site ↗
14

McKinney Falls State Park

Southeast Austin · 13 miles from the Capitol

$6 · gates 8am–10pm · day-pass reservations recommended

“Find solitude and beauty in Austin’s city limits,” says TPWD, and it delivers. The Rock Shelter Trail is 0.6 miles past a 100-foot bald cypress called Old Baldy; the Onion Creek Trail is 2.8.

Official site ↗
15

Enchanted Rock

Fredericksburg

$8 · reserve a pass, January is busy season · trails open 30 minutes before sunrise

The Summit Trail at first light, the whole Hill Country going pink underneath you. The biggest outing here; pack your own water and make a day of it.

Official site ↗
Part VI · 1 place

Alone, on Purpose

Go by yourself. Book a single tee time and see who you get paired with, or nobody. Soak somewhere no one needs anything from you. The holidays are a month of other people’s plans, and the reset is the one plan that is only yours.

16

Lake Austin Spa Resort, LakeHouse Spa

Lake Austin

Spa day pass $150 Mon–Thu, $225 Fri–Sun, plus service charge and tax · adults only · book by phone, 512.372.7380

Pool, whirlpool, steam room, dry sauna, quiet rooms, lunch and a robe. The resort lists the pass as ideal for “solo retreats,” which is the only endorsement this post needs.

Official site ↗
Part VII · 1 place

One Small Luxury

Almost everything here is cheap or free, so spend in one place and make it count: the full day at Ottine, the LakeHouse Spa pass, or a first-tee-time morning at Lost Pines with the sauna after.

17

Lost Pines Golf Club

Bastrop · about 20 minutes

Public tee times online; the rate shows at booking · Arthur Hills, 7,300 yards, par 72

Still Wolfdancer to most of us. The course runs from open pasture down into the loblolly pines and the Colorado River bottom, and the resort singles out the 603-yard 3rd and the downhill 155-yard 12th. Book the first tee time, then the resort spa’s sauna. More in our special day rounds.

Official site ↗
After

Warm Food, Slow Coffee

Eat something warm, drink your coffee sitting down, and don’t take a call. We have already done the work on where: The Morning Round pairs a café and a lunch with every muni in town, and our Austin coffee guide covers the rest.

The Last Word

Do Less, on Purpose

None of this needs a plan. Pick one soak, one walk and one round, do them on a weekday, and leave the phone in the car for at least one of them. That is the whole reset. Prices and hours were checked on official sites on November 22, 2026; Deep Eddy Pool is closed for maintenance from November 30, so we left it out. If you have a place that belongs on this list, tell us about it.
