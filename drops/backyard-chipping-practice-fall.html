Backyard Chipping, Fall Edition

By Lenny Harrington, Founder & Editor · Updated November 18, 2026

The Kit · Backyard chipping25 picks + a game · in stock, checked November 18, 2026
The TGI Take

We put a practice mat in the backyard this fall, and it changed how evenings go. Twenty minutes of chips before dinner, a hoodie on once the sun drops behind the fence, nobody keeping score unless somebody wants to. This post is everything that makes that setup better: tools that tell you something, wedges that are a pleasure to hold, clothes you would wear anyway, and a game to keep it from getting boring.

Start with feedback, then targets. The Divot Board shows you where the club meets the ground, which is the whole game when you are chipping off a mat. After that, give yourself targets at three distances. EyeLine’s rings and a couple of flags are enough to play Nine Inside 50 every night of the week, and that game is built around the two things that make you deadly inside 50 yards: distance control and trajectory control.

Wedges. You do not need a new set to chip in the yard, but if one wedge is going to change, this is a good place to try it. Miura’s raw C grind will age with you, Bettinardi’s bronze HLX looks like the season, and STIX’s 60 is the most affordable way to add a lob wedge.

What to wear. Six hoodies and six pairs of gym shorts, chosen as much for the photos as the fabric. Fall in the yard means a warm afternoon and a cold evening, often on the same day, so the combination makes sense.

What is in here. 25 pieces from 22 brands, none of them featured on TGI before. All were in stock when we checked on November 18, 2026, at the prices each store shows a US visitor; the Malbon short was last checked on November 16.

The game

Nine Inside 50

Two skills, nine shotsThe goal is to be deadly inside 50 yards, and that comes down to two things: how far the ball goes and how high it flies. This game tests both in about ten minutes.

Setup. Put the mat at one end of the yard and a flag or target at three distances: about 10, 20 and 30 yards. Lay a ring around each target. On the weekend, take the same game to the short-game area and stretch the last target to 50 yards.

Play. Hit three shots to each target, and call the height before every one: low, middle or high. Use the same wedge for all nine and change the trajectory by moving the ball in your stance: back foot for low, center for middle, front foot for high.

Score. One point if the ball flies the height you called. One point if it finishes inside the ring. Nine shots, 18 points possible.

Keep the number. Write your score down every time and try to beat it. When you clear 12 regularly, move the targets back.

The Last Word

Ten Minutes Before Dinner

The best thing about a backyard setup is how little it asks of you. No tee time, no drive, no range bucket. Walk out, hit nine chips, write down a number and go back inside.

Do that a few nights a week through the fall and you will feel it the next time you are short-sided on a real course.
