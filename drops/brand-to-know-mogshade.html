Brand to Know — Mogshade

mogshadegolf.com 17 Pieces Updated August 2026

Mogshade makes golf headcovers by hand in Portugal, and the name tells you everything about the sensibility: it comes from an old English dialect word for the play of light and shadow — sun filtering through leaves onto the course. The whole operation runs on what the brand calls ethical circularity. Everything is made to order (current lead time is about three weeks), which means no overproduction, and the fabrics are natural, recycled, upcycled, offcut, or deadstock — including rescued wool from the Serra da Estrela mountain factories that anchors the Re.Weave and Churra collections. Every piece is slightly different because the fabric was never mass-produced; what you get is what's left from the loom.

Since we first covered them in June, the catalog has kept moving — small handmade batches sell out fast here, which is half the appeal. The Depeche Golf magazine collab dropped in August with a special-edition headcover. The wall art program is real now: hand-tufted wool posters of imagined holes and pin sheets that take eight weeks to make. There's an Ocean Bottle collab, sweats, and socks. And for American readers, the practical note: since June, US orders ship with import duties and customs fees already included.

The other thing that sets Mogshade apart is how much company it keeps. For a one-workshop operation, the collab sheet reads like a map of independent golf: an artist series with Fabricaal that turned headcovers into canvases, a capsule with IGG that sold through in weeks, Projeto Serra — a tribute to the brand's home mountains, down to the "Ovelha Preta" black-sheep cover — and an "On Set with Manors" edit curated with the London golfwear brand. Add the Depeche Golf magazine bundle, the Ocean Bottle, a Fiumani cap, and the early Parmore run that was capped at 50 pieces, and you get the picture: almost everything they touch disappears. There's also a custom-made program if you want a cover built to your spec from whatever's on the shelf that month.

Mogshade — Frequently Asked

What does the name Mogshade mean?

Mogshade comes from an old English dialect word describing the interplay of light and shadow - like sunlight filtering through leaves and branches onto the course. The brand chose it to capture those quiet moments on a golf course, and its woven patterns play on the same idea.

Where are Mogshade headcovers made?

Every Mogshade piece is handmade in Portugal. Headcovers are made to order with a current lead time of roughly three weeks, which is central to the brand's no-overproduction model.

What materials does Mogshade use?

Mogshade builds around what it calls ethical circularity: natural, recycled, upcycled, offcut, and deadstock materials. The Re.Weave and Churra collections use rescued high-quality wool fabrics from factories in Portugal's Serra da Estrela mountains.

What is the Churra collection?

Churra is Mogshade's textured wool line named after the Churra sheep breed native to the Serra da Estrela region. The covers are made from rescued mountain-woven fabrics with a handwoven character you can feel.

Does Mogshade make anything besides headcovers?

Yes - golf towels, cases and pouches, ball markers, clothing, an Ocean Bottle collaboration, and a wall art line of hand-tufted wool posters of imagined golf holes that ship in about eight weeks.

What collabs has Mogshade done?

Mogshade has collaborated with the artist Fabricaal, IGG, Projeto Serra, the London golfwear brand Manors (the On Set with Manors edit), Depeche Golf magazine, Ocean Bottle, Fiumani, and Parmore. Most collaboration pieces sold out quickly.

Do US orders pay import duties on Mogshade?

No. Since June 2026, Mogshade includes import duties and customs handling fees in the price for US orders, so there are no surprise charges at delivery.
