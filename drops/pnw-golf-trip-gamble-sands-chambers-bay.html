Field Note: My 2027 Golf Trip to the Pacific Northwest

By Lenny Harrington · Updated October 12, 2026

Washington and Oregon5 days · 6 rounds · checked October 12, 2026Hero photo courtesy of Gamble Sands
The TGI Take

I am setting my intention for 2027: a golf trip to the Pacific Northwest. Chambers Bay and Gamble Sands have been on my list for years, and I am writing the plan down so it actually happens. Nothing is booked yet and all of it is flexible, but here is what is on our radar: five days, two states and six rounds, from the sand dunes of eastern Washington to a hilly muni outside Portland.

The rough shape: start at Gamble Sands, David McLay Kidd’s resort on a bluff above the Columbia River, for 36 holes and a night on the putting course. Cross the Cascades past Lake Chelan and Leavenworth to Tacoma for The Home Course and Chambers Bay, with Gold Mountain as a bonus round if our legs allow. Finish in Portland at Wildwood, with one big dinner and a waterfall on the way to the airport.

We are leaning toward September. Gamble Sands is still at its peak, Chambers Bay is cheaper than in high summer, the rain usually has not arrived west of the mountains, and after Labor Day the Multnomah Falls permit season is over. The thinking is to fly into Wenatchee and out of Portland.

How we would book it. The Inn at Gamble Sands first: overnight guests can already book every 2027 date, room and tee times together. Then Chambers Bay, where non-resident advance times opened on January 1 in 2026. Then Le Pigeon and everything else. Green fees for the six rounds come to roughly $1,200 to $1,400 a person before lodging, using 2027 rates at Gamble Sands and 2026 rates elsewhere. Stops will move around, and I will update this as the plan firms up.

Day 1: Arrive at Gamble Sands

Brewster, WashingtonThe idea: fly into Wenatchee, check in, play the short course at sunset and putt by firelight.

1/4
Day 1 · Brewster, Washington
The Inn at Gamble Sands · From $475 a night, May–Oct 2027
The plan is to fly Seattle to Wenatchee on Alaska, about 90 minutes from the resort, though a four-hour drive from Sea-Tac is the backup. We want to stay on site: every room has a fireplace, the 40 Golf View rooms look over Scarecrow, and overnight guests can already book tee times for every 2027 date, while day visitors only get 120 days out. That alone makes the case.

1/3
Day 1, late afternoon
QuickSands · $80 in 2027
Fourteen short holes on the bluff with a pull cart and a couple of wedges. After a travel day, this is the round we have in mind first: no pressure, big views over the Columbia River, and a chance to get our eyes in before the big courses. Ideally as the light drops.

Day 1, after dark
The Cascades Putting Course · At the resort
A 100,000-square-foot putting course, 175 yards end to end, that the resort calls an homage to the Himalayas at St Andrews. There are fire pits, and after dark they hand out glow balls. This is where we figure the first bet of the trip gets settled.

Day 1 dinner · on site
The Barn · All day
The casual one: pizzas, sliders and a canning machine that seals cocktails to go. A patio dinner, the sun going down over the river and an early night is the idea. The next day is 36 holes.

Day 2: Thirty-Six Holes Above the Columbia

The two big coursesGamble Sands in the morning, Scarecrow in the afternoon at the second-18 rate, then steak.

1/4
Day 2 morning
Gamble Sands · $315 May–Sep 2027, cart included
David McLay Kidd's course on a sandy bluff above the Columbia, with fairways so wide and greens so receptive that the whole design is about fun rather than punishment. We are thinking a morning tee time, while the wind is down. If the dates slide, April and October are shoulder season at $235.
Tee times ↗
1/4
Day 2 afternoon
Scarecrow · $210 as a second 18
The second course, designed by McLay Kidd with Nick Schaan and opened slightly more than a decade after the first. It is wilder than the original, and the second-18 rate would make an afternoon round the best value of the trip. If we only have legs for one more 18 all week, this is the one.
Tee times ↗
1/4
Day 2 dinner · on site
Danny Boy · Dinner only
The resort's steakhouse: prime rib, ribeye and the Tower of Tots, with a sommelier on site. After 36 holes in the desert it feels earned. We will try to book the table when we book the room.

Day 3: Over the Cascades

Lake Chelan, Leavenworth, Stevens PassFour and a half hours of driving with the best stops in the state, then a twilight round in DuPont.

1/2
Day 3 morning · Lake Chelan
Tsillan Cellars and Sorrento’s Ristorante · South Shore
The drive west passes the south shore of Lake Chelan, 35 miles from the resort. Tsillan is an estate winery with an Italian restaurant on the same property, under new local owners as of July 2026. It is on our list as a stop for the view and a bottle for the car.
Visit ↗
1/1
Day 3 lunch · Leavenworth
München Haus · No reservations
Halfway over Stevens Pass is Leavenworth, a whole town built to look Bavarian. München Haus is its sausage grill and beer garden, with Icicle Brewing beers on tap. A good place to eat outside and stretch our legs before getting back on US-2.
Visit ↗
1/6
Day 3 twilight · DuPont
The Home Course · $64–$70, twilight $54–$56
Owned and run by the Pacific Northwest Golf Association and Washington Golf, and laid out for walking with views of Puget Sound, the Olympics and Mount Rainier. It would be the cheapest great round of the week and is about 20 minutes from Chambers Bay. The idea is to roll in off the pass and catch a twilight time. Rates read in October 2026; the association rate applies to Oregon Golf Association members.

Day 3 dinner · Point Ruston
Copper & Salt Northwest Kitchen · Ruston waterfront
Italian-leaning Northwest cooking on the Ruston waterfront, recommended by Chambers Bay itself, with Point Defiance Park next door. It books on OpenTable. Staying nearby would put us about 15 minutes from the first tee in the morning.

Day 4: Chambers Bay

The U.S. Open course, then the bonusChambers Bay with caddies in the morning. If we still have it in us, Gold Mountain after lunch, then dinner on the harbor.

1/4
Day 4 morning · University Place
Chambers Bay · $279 non-resident in Sep 2026, plus 15.2% tax
The reason for the trip. Robert Trent Jones II built it on an old sand and gravel mine above Puget Sound, and it hosted the 2015 U.S. Open. It is walking only, and we plan to take caddies ($100 a bag plus tip) and budget five hours. Non-resident advance times for April to October 2026 opened on January 1; if 2027 works the same way, that is the alarm to set.
Rates and booking ↗
1/3
Day 4 afternoon · the bonus round
Gold Mountain Golf Club, Olympic Course · Dynamic pricing
If our legs survive Chambers, the idea is to drive 40 minutes across the Narrows to Bremerton. The Olympic is a city-owned course through tall firs that hosted the 2006 U.S. Amateur Public Links, and Chambers Bay bundles it into its own package with The Home Course. Rates move with demand, so this one gets decided closer to the day.
Green fees ↗
1/2
Day 4 dinner · Gig Harbor
Tides Tavern · 21 and over
On the way back from Bremerton, a waterfront tavern in a 1910 building that has been pouring beer since 1973, with Mount Rainier across the harbor on a clear night. It is now part of the Anthony's family, it is 21 and over, and its own site lists two sets of hours, so we will call ahead.

Day 5: Portland

Biscuits, a hilly muni and the big dinnerDown I-5 to Oregon for one last round, then a waterfall on the way to the airport.

1/3
Day 5 brunch · Portland
Pine State Biscuits · Daily 8am–2pm
Two hours and change down I-5 would put us in Portland in time for the Reggie Deluxe: fried chicken, bacon, cheddar and an egg on a biscuit, covered in gravy. There are four locations, so we will hit whichever is on the way to the course.
Visit ↗
1/1
Day 5 afternoon · NW St. Helens Rd
Wildwood Golf Course · Call for rates
The plan is to finish small. Wildwood is a hilly public course from 1990, 15 miles north of downtown off Highway 30, with creeks running through it and the Cascade volcanoes on the skyline. It is short, around 5,850 yards, and the right last round to play for pride. It does not have a working website, so it is a phone call to (503) 621-3402 to book.

The Questions

When is the best time for a Pacific Northwest golf trip?

September. Gamble Sands is still in its peak season, Chambers Bay's non-resident rate drops from $325 in July and August to $279 in September (2026 rates), it is usually drier west of the Cascades, and the Multnomah Falls timed-permit season ended on September 7 in 2026.

How far is Gamble Sands from Seattle?

About 221 miles and four hours by car, according to the resort. Wenatchee airport, with Alaska flights from Seattle, is 77 miles and about 90 minutes away.

How much does it cost to play Chambers Bay?

Non-resident rates in 2026 started at $220 in April, $299 in June, $325 in July and August, $279 in September and $229 in October, plus 15.2% tax. Caddies are $100 a bag plus tip. 2027 rates had not been published on October 12, 2026.

Is Chambers Bay walking only?

Yes. Push carts are free and caddies are available; motorized carts are only for golfers with a medical need.

How much does Gamble Sands cost in 2027?

Gamble Sands or Scarecrow is $315 from May to September 2027 and $235 in April and October, with a second 18 the same day at $210 in peak season. QuickSands is $80. Cart included; taxes extra.

Who owns The Home Course?

It is cooperatively owned and operated by the Pacific Northwest Golf Association and Washington Golf. Rates in October 2026 were $64 Monday to Thursday and $70 Friday to Sunday for 18 holes.
