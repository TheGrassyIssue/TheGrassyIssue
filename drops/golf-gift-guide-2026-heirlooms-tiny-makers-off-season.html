The TGI Take

Part 3 closes the guide with the gifts that take the longest view. Things built to outlast the person who opens them, things made by one or two people at a bench, and the layers that get a golfer through the months when the course is cold. Same format as Parts 1 and 2: each brand gets its own block, and none of the ninety brands across the guide appears twice.

Thirty brands, from an $11 hammered ball marker out of Wodonga to a $2,700 all-leather Sunday bag from Colombia. Several of these makers work to order, so the earlier you buy, the better.

Where to start. For something to hand down, Sun Mountain’s Legacy leather stand bag. For a tiny maker, a hand-knit set from AustinKnittyLimits. For the off-season, SLOAT’s organic Ventile jacket.

The Last Word

Order Early, Then Wait

The gifts in this part reward patience. Leather gets better with use, small makers work through a queue, and a good winter layer earns its place in February, not on Christmas morning.

Check the timing. Artisan’s putters and irons and CMD’s bespoke putters start with a deposit and a build. Steurer & Jacoby asks four to six weeks for handmade pieces. Left of Field is on sale now, and MacKenzie collaborations are limited runs, so neither will wait long.

Prices are as each store shows them to a US visitor. Late Nine, Odd Ritual, Local GC and Northern Ball Markers show their own currencies, so those dollar figures are approximate. Everything was available to order on November 9, 2026. Part 1 and Part 2 cover the other six categories.
