Brand to Know — Sentinel Golf

sentinelgolf.us Minneapolis, MN 12 Pieces

Sentinel Golf is a product design lab based in Minneapolis that builds things no other golf brand would attempt. Founded on the principle “might as well make it,” Sentinel collaborates with manufacturers across industries — Danish leather houses, Kermit Chair Company in Nashville, Dyneema engineers, Cerakote coaters — to introduce ideas both foreign and forgotten to golf. The brand operates on a limited pre-order model: when a production window opens, you buy or you miss it. No restocks, no discounts, no unaccounted-for stock. It keeps the waste low and the demand permanent.

What makes Sentinel different isn’t the price points — though those range from $38 socks to a $3,900 geodesic dome tent — it’s the conviction. A hand-stitched Sørensen Walker carry bag at $1,750. A Dyneema rain suit at $540. An alignment stick with a Cerakote ceramic finish. A collapsible Kermit Chair re-skinned in ultralight performance fabric. These aren’t products designed to sell in volume. They’re designed to exist, and the people who find them tend to keep them forever. The sold-out archive is the real collection — and it reads like a design museum catalog.

The Story — FAQ
Who is behind Sentinel Golf?
Sentinel Golf is the work of John Mooty, who runs the brand out of Minneapolis. He built it as a product design lab rather than a traditional golf company — a way to experiment, discover, and reimagine, pulling in manufacturers from outside the golf world to make things golf has either never seen or long forgotten.
What is Sentinel Golf's design philosophy?
The founding principle is “might as well make it.” Sentinel chases ideas rooted in quality and substance, simplicity and utility — objects designed to exist rather than to sell in volume. Its take on the MacKenzie carry bag captures the approach: a design “born fully formed,” refined over the years “akin to a sculpture,” with every unnecessary element stripped away.
Who does Sentinel Golf collaborate with?
Sentinel's collaborator list reads like a design directory: Sørensen, the Danish eco-conscious leather house, for its hand-stitched Walker bags; FPS, a Tokyo label founded by husband and wife Tomio and Yukiko, for leather blade covers; Kvadrat, the Danish textile maker, for headcovers knit in Denmark and sewn in New York; Anderson's, making woven belts in Parma, Italy for fifty years; the Kermit Chair Company in Nashville; and 1733.
Why is everything limited pre-order?
Sentinel produces in pre-order windows to, in its own words, “control costs, lower risk, and limit waste without the need to discount unaccounted-for stock.” When a window opens you buy or you miss it — no restocks, no leftover inventory. The sold-out archive becomes the brand's museum.
What inspired Sentinel's Basecamp series?
Founder John Mooty's trips to Minnesota's Boundary Waters — over a million acres of protected wilderness where you camp, canoe, and fish with no cell service. He told The Old Ghosts the series tries to capture that feeling in the form of a golf trip. The second Basecamp Expedition capsule, shot at Sand Valley, spans rain gear, lightweight carry bags, tents, and accessories that bridge camping, golf, and fishing — the reason writer Michael Williams calls Sentinel "an American Snow Peak, but for golf."
Is Sentinel Golf gear made in the USA?
Much of it is, and domestic manufacturing matters to the brand — but Mooty says it isn't an absolute requirement. What he looks for are the people who care most about what they make and use the best inputs they can find, whether that's in the US, Japan, or Europe. His own inspirations run well outside golf: F/CE, Norda, Saunders Militaria, and Foreign Rider.
Where can I buy Sentinel Golf?
Directly at sentinelgolf.us during open pre-order windows. Follow the drops closely — windows close fast, and pieces rarely return.
