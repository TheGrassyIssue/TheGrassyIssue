Feather & Hide: Reviving the Old Culture of Golf, One Caddie Tee at a Time

By Lenny Harrington · Updated November 8, 2026

Brand to Know · Revive the Game12 picks · in stock, checked November 8, 2026
The TGI Take

Feather & Hide says it is “committing to the culture of historical golf. Not a trend.” We read the name as a nod to the featherie, golf’s first ball, made by stuffing feathers into a leather hide, and their About page describes golf as an arduous love-hate relationship. Their line is “Revive the Game.”

What we like most is how honest it is. The founder, who signs his letters “Lou,” writes a candid newsletter called the Sunday Letter. In July he wrote about the company’s third year: moves, a return to a day job, a death in the family and a wedding, then placing the largest inventory order in the brand’s history and, in his words, biting his nails down to the nubs. It reads like a conversation over a beer, which is exactly what he says he was going for.

The clothes back up the attitude. The Ranger polo and the Honours hoodie are good, plain performance pieces with a small logo, and the graphic tees stand up for caddies and grounds crews instead of the clubhouse. “Carts are overrated and overpriced” is not a line you see from many golf brands.

Where to start. The Ranger Golf Polo, $65, in pine green or dusk, and the Support Your Local Caddie hat, $32. If you want the whole story in one box, look at Members Only below.

Members Only

A Golf Box Four Times a Year

Feather & Hide’s membership is $300 a year for four seasonal boxes, arriving in April, July, October and January. Each one has exclusive Feather & Hide apparel, a print from golf photographer RJ Gilbert and a golf artwork by Claire Nilan, and members get 15% off the site all year. The 2026 window closed on April 10; it is a good one to watch for 2027, and an easy gift for the golfer who already has everything.

The Last Word

Go Low

Feather & Hide is a small brand still finding its feet. Lou compares it to a baby horse taking its first steps: standing up, a little wobbly. That honesty is the reason to pay attention. The brand is working on collegiate licensing, a Salty Golfer collab came out of a single round, and new colors of the Ranger, the Starter and the Honours keep arriving.

Buy the polo because it is a good polo. Buy the caddie tee because you agree with it. And read the Sunday Letter, because there are not many golf brands writing to their customers like that. Every letter ends the same way: “Go low.”
