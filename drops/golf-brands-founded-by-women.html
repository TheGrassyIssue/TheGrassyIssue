Seventeen Golf Brands Founded by Women

17 Brands·Men’s and unisex · Founders named · Stores checked Aug 2026

The argument for this group is not who founded the brands but how well the things are made, and Jan Craig is the proof. In 1962 a golfer of that name, out on a rural route outside Cincinnati, made her own headcovers in worsted wool because she could not buy any she liked for a new set of clubs. Jack Nicklaus ordered a set within a few years, Tom Watson carried them, and so did multiple Ryder Cup teams. Every cover is still hand-knit to order, and the only design change in six decades was elastic inside the driver.

The animal headcover came seventeen years later, from a mother and daughter in Phoenix. Frank, the tiger, went on Tiger Woods’ driver at the 1997 Masters, Nike licensed the character and named him, and the business grew four hundred per cent in a quarter.

Neither of those is a novelty company, and neither is interesting only for who started it. Seventeen brands do both things at once: founded or co-founded by a woman, and making what men buy, which is headcovers, leather, bags, gloves, towels, socks, polos and shoes. Almost every women-founded golf brand makes women’s apparel and nothing else, and almost every independent brand making unisex hard goods was founded by men, so the overlap between the two runs thin.

One pattern turns up again and again. Megan Chisti co-founded Seamus, trained as the designer, rebuilt the headcover that started the company and still sews product, and there is no published quote from her anywhere. Gail Hanson co-founded Bluetross, and the first prototypes came off her inherited sewing machine; the biggest feature ever written about the company is headlined “the unlikely story of a man who quit his job to make leather headcovers.” Neither brand is hiding anything, and both credit her plainly on their own About page. The voice just defaults to him. So every entry names the woman, and says plainly when she co-founded with a husband or a mother rather than rounding up.

The Makers

Six · headcovers and leatherThese are the brands where a woman is the one cutting and sewing the product: a hand-knitting operation running since 1962, a Pendleton designer working in factory scrap, and a leather shop built on an inherited sewing machine.

1/6
Jan Craig, founder · Janet Craig Cruise, third generation
Jan Craig Headcovers · Hand-knit since 1962
Jan was an avid golfer who could not find covers she liked, so she knitted her own on a rural route outside Cincinnati, shared party line and all. Jack Nicklaus ordered a set in the mid-sixties, and Tom Watson and multiple Ryder Cup teams carried them after him. They are still hand-knit to order in 100% worsted wool, $63 to $79, and the only design change in six decades was elastic inside the driver covers.
Visit Jan Craig Headcovers ↗
1/6
Megan Chisti, co-founder with her husband Akbar
Seamus Golf · Oregon wool, Scottish mills
A Royal Troon headcover Akbar had been given wore out in 2011, and Megan, a womenswear designer at Pendleton Woolen Mills, rebuilt it in a garage from factory remnants. The company is made of Pendleton scrap, and Pendleton became a licensed partner rather than the other way round. The first order was Bandon Dunes. Covers run $110 to $140 in wool, leather, waxed canvas and tweed, and the company registers as women and minority owned.
Visit Seamus Golf ↗
1/6
Gail Hanson, co-founder with her husband Marc
Bluetross · Horween leather, Charleston
Marc wanted to turn a pin flag from Newport Country Club into a headcover for his brother, and the flag still hangs unmade in the production facility while the company it accidentally spawned sells $225 versions of the idea. The first prototypes came off Gail’s inherited sewing machine, which Marc destroyed inside a month. Italian vachetta and Horween leather patina rather than wear out, and the range runs to belts, wallets and scorecard covers.
Visit Bluetross ↗
1/6
Jane Spicer, co-founder with her mother Daphne
Daphne’s Headcovers · Est. 1979
Daphne’s invented the animal headcover and made Frank, the tiger Tiger Woods first carried at the 1997 Masters, after which business grew 400% in a quarter. Nike licensed the character and named him; Spicer’s one condition was that he stay likable, because her late mother’s actual signature is stitched into the label of every cover the company makes. A hundred-plus designs sit at a flat $45.99, from wolf and shark to Nicklaus’s Golden Bear.
Visit Daphne’s Headcovers ↗
1/6
Paige Harding, co-founder with her partner Adam Brown
Mackem Golf · Perth, Western Australia
Mackem is slang for a native of Sunderland, which is where Adam is from. Paige has the textiles background and does the pattern-making and construction, and Adam calls her the brains and the brawn of it. Everything is cut and sewn in their own Kalamunda studio in wool, houndstooth, corduroy, waxed canvas and tartan, with a further line built from reclaimed materials. Covers run A$85 to A$95, pouches to A$125.
Visit Mackem Golf ↗
1/6
Zoe Walker-Faure, founder
Zalea’s Golf Co. · Hand-sewn, Palm Desert
Zalea’s does not market to men at all. The homepage reads Headcovers Made for HER GAME and every description names a lady golfer, but the gingham and stripe patterns are neutral enough to sit on anyone’s bag. It started as a dorm-room Etsy shop and the covers are still cut by Zoe’s mother and sewn by her grandmother, with every print drawn by Zoe herself. Twice a year the scrap becomes one-off covers, at $70.
Visit Zalea’s Golf Co. ↗
“Well, you know, I think that business is just like hiking, really… Some days it’s great, you’re on top of the summit. Some days it’s rainy and muddy, and it stinks. Some days you’re banged up; some days you’re on top of the world. But if you continue going, it’s always worthwhile.”— Jane Spicer, CEO, Daphne’s Headcovers

The Outfitters

Five · bags, gloves, towels, socksThese are the things that hang off the bag or go in it. Two have been trading since the seventies, and one names no founder anywhere on its own site, leaving a Companies House filing as the only public record.

1/5
Deborah and Erica Bennett, married co-founders
ORKAI · Bags, formerly ORCA Golf
Deborah Bennett is CEO and Erica Bennett president and chief designer, and they built it together. ORKAI was official bag supplier to all eight teams at the 2023 LPGA International Crown, hand-finished in Boca Raton in vegan Nappa rather than hide. They rebranded from ORCA Golf in January 2026 to push into travel and lifestyle, keeping the orca’s fluke as the mark because no two are alike. Bags start at $275, bespoke commissions to $1,580.
Visit ORKAI ↗
1/6
Meagan Ouderkirk and Antonia DiPaolo, co-founders
Hedge · New York and the Hamptons
Hedge came out of frustration with kit designed mostly by men, on a tennis court at the Bridgehampton Club; Ouderkirk came from Ralph Lauren. The range is the strangest in golf: a $99.99 Sunday bag and $29.99 Cabretta gloves next to a caddie brooch and a stroke-counter bracelet handmade by a named jeweller. Their Criquet collab began as a joke at the 2018 PGA Show about the two brands dating, made real a year later.
Visit Hedge ↗
1/6
Christina Rogers, co-founder with Mark Williams
Sinking Birdies · Hampshire, England
The prints are bold graphics on the things you lose: AAA Cabretta gloves at £18, magnetic microfibre towels, PU driver and mallet covers, bamboo tees and a leather glove case, with no florals anywhere. The brand’s own site names no founder, and neither does the one piece of UK trade press about it. Christina Rogers is on record in exactly one place, a Companies House filing dated 4 July 2023, the day it incorporated.
Visit Sinking Birdies ↗
1/6
Mary Ann Sheppard, founder
Devant Sport Towels · Fifty years old this year
Mary Ann started Devant in 1976 by taking the small short-run orders a big contract mill could not be bothered with. It now makes over a million towels a year in Pageland, South Carolina, under USGA, PGA of America, PGA Tour and LPGA licences, so the towel on the bag at the U.S. Open traces back to her. Fifty years of continuous trading is close to unheard of for a woman-founded hard-goods company.
Visit Devant Sport Towels ↗
1/6
Isabelle Shee, co-founder
Inside Story · Coffee grounds and bottles
Shee was a college golfer at UC Riverside and then UNLV, and picked up the nickname Sock Girl for wearing over-the-knee socks as sun protection, a teenage workaround for her mother’s sunscreen rules. She co-founded the company in 2019 with Greg Ashton and her mother Kat. The yarn is nano-particled coffee grounds plus recycled bottle fibre, about 70% upcycled, and carries a four-year no-hole guarantee. Men’s crew and calf start at $18.
Visit Inside Story Socks ↗
“ORKAI started in my studio, with one bag, one client, one story. Every piece since has carried that same intention — designed by hand, made for one. The brand has grown. The standard hasn’t moved.”— Erica Bennett, co-founder and chief designer, ORKAI

The Labels

Six · apparel and footwearThese brands cut an actual men’s line. One of them sells nothing else, one started as a camper towed across America, and one is an Italian shoe house that has not been owned by its founders for a decade.

1/5
Fiona Cohen, founder
Kingfisher Golf · Dallas menswear
Cohen is a woman designing men’s clothes, with no women’s line at all. She was an art director at PepsiCo before she started Kingfisher at a kitchen counter in East Dallas with a sewing machine and thrift-store fabric. Tees are screen-printed at a shop in Old East Dallas at $40; the $65 polos come from the same factories as the big performance brands. Its breakout was a citywide open call to find Dallas’s worst golfer.
Visit Kingfisher Golf ↗
1/6
Kristina Loftus, co-founder with her husband Matt and Kevin Hubbard
Rhoback · Charlottesville, Virginia
Kristina was the first of the three to go full time, spending the brand’s first two years towing a pop-up cross-country and selling shirts out of parking lots, 25,000 miles of it. She built the original website, teaching herself the software to do it. The name comes from their Rhodesian Ridgeback, and the two vertical stripes on the back of every piece are the ridge. Men’s performance polos are $98, vests $154, quarter-zips $145.
Visit Rhoback ↗
1/6
Erica Malbon, co-founder with her husband Stephen
Malbon Golf · Los Angeles
Malbon began as an Instagram mood board, and Erica arrived having co-founded The Now, an LA massage business, with no background in sport or retail. The brand launched menswear first in 2017 and did not add a proper women’s line until 2023; by her own account men’s is where the market was. She is co-chief creative officer today, a title she kept when an outside CEO arrived. Tees are $68, polos $108 to $148.
Visit Malbon Golf ↗
1/6
Tamasine Green, co-founder with Robert Hart
Bunker Mentality · Nottinghamshire, England
Bunker Mentality has been independent for more than twenty years, which almost nothing in the category manages. The interesting part is recent: they abandoned stocked inventory and moved to made-to-order, so each garment is printed, cut and sewn only once you have bought it and ships straight from a UK factory, which leaves no warehouse and no discount cycle. Twenty-four sizes across three body lengths come as standard; men’s polos are £70, gilets £110.
Visit Bunker Mentality ↗
1/6
Alex Bartholomew, sole founder
Royal Albartross · Italian and Portuguese leather
Alex Bartholomew built one of very few luxury footwear houses in golf founded by a woman, and she trained for it: fashion and textiles at Brighton, shoemaking at Cordwainers, LK Bennett, Burberry and the British Leather Technology Centre. The shoes are made in small family-run factories in Italy and Portugal on Vibram soles. The name is a love letter in plain sight: Al Bart for Alex Bartholomew, Ross for her husband, around the golf term.
Visit Royal Albartross ↗
1/6
Antje Elle, co-founder with Baldovino Mattiazzo
Duca del Cosma · Founded 2004, since sold twice
Antje Elle trained at the Munich School for Fashion Graphic Design and co-founded Duca del Cosma in 2004 with the Venetian designer Baldovino Mattiazzo; the name means Duke of Cosma, from his family. Neither owns it now: a Dutch shoe-industry veteran bought it around 2015, a Dubai investment firm in May 2026. A house selling twenty years of Italian heritage has not been owned by either founder for a decade; men’s shoes start at $100.
Visit Duca del Cosma ↗
“I was an awful employee. I can’t tell you how many times my ‘washing machine flooded’ and I was ridiculously late for work. I simply could not motivate myself to go to work for anyone else — even in my 20’s.”— Alex Bartholomew, founder, Royal Albartross
Which golf brands are founded by women?

Seventeen are covered here, all currently trading with stock: Jan Craig Headcovers, Seamus Golf, Bluetross, Daphne's Headcovers, Mackem Golf, Zalea's Golf Co., ORKAI, Hedge, Sinking Birdies, Devant Sport Towels, Inside Story Socks, Kingfisher Golf, Rhoback, Malbon Golf, Bunker Mentality, Royal Albartross and Duca del Cosma. Each founder is named from a brand-owned page, a signed interview or a company filing.

Are there women-founded golf brands that make men's clothing?

Yes, six of them here. Kingfisher Golf makes men's polos and tees and nothing else — founder Fiona Cohen designs no women's line at all. Rhoback, Malbon and Bunker Mentality all run full men's ranges, Royal Albartross makes men's leather golf shoes, and Duca del Cosma makes men's Italian footwear.

Who made Tiger Woods' tiger headcover?

Daphne's Headcovers, the Phoenix company Jane Spicer started with her mother Daphne in 1979. Woods first carried it at the 1997 Masters. Nike later licensed the character and named him Frank. Daphne's covers are a flat $45.99.

What is the oldest women-founded golf brand?

Jan Craig Headcovers, founded in 1962 by Jan Craig, who knitted her own covers because she could not find any she liked. Jack Nicklaus ordered a set in the mid-1960s. Devant Sport Towels is next at 1976, and Daphne's at 1979.

Is Seamus Golf women-owned?

Partly. Megan Chisti co-founded it in 2011 with her husband Akbar, and the company registers as women and minority owned. Megan was a womenswear designer at Pendleton Woolen Mills and rebuilt the original headcover from Pendleton remnants — though nearly every published quote about the company is Akbar's.

Which of these brands make headcovers?

Six. Jan Craig hand-knits wool covers to order. Seamus works in Scottish wool, leather and waxed canvas. Bluetross uses Horween and Italian leather. Daphne's makes the novelty animals. Mackem Golf sews wool and corduroy in Perth. Sinking Birdies does bold-print PU.

Do any of these brands make golf bags?

Two. ORKAI, founded by Deborah and Erica Bennett, makes stand and cart bags from $275 and was the official bag supplier to all eight teams at the 2023 LPGA International Crown. Hedge makes a $99.99 Sunday bag.

Why isn't Rose & Fire on this list?

It is not women-owned, though it is very often assumed to be. It was founded by Mike Buchfuhrer. Rose was his grandmother, who designed hats after the Second World War, and Fire is a play on his surname.

What happened to KINONA and PRIO Golf?

Both appear in most articles on this subject and neither has a working store. KINONA's domain redirects to an unrelated site and PRIO's has lapsed to a parking page. LIJA and Jamie Sadock don't resolve at all. That is why this list was rebuilt from live stores rather than from other lists.

How were these brands verified?

Every founder had to be named in a source we opened — a signed About page, a named interview, or in one case a Companies House filing — rather than inferred from a brand seeming women-led. Every store was checked for live stock. Birds of Condor was cut because its own About page names no founder and only one local paper credits a woman co-founder.

Where should I start if I only buy one thing?

An object with history: a Jan Craig hybrid cover at $63, hand-knit the same way since 1962. Cheapest way in: Inside Story men's calf socks at $18, or a Devant towel at $12.95. For clothing: a Kingfisher polo at $65.
