Far and Wide, Vol. 2: Six More Golf Brands From Around the World, and Where to Play in Their Backyard

By Lenny Harrington, Founder & Editor · Updated November 19, 2026

The Edit · Six countries, six courses35 picks · 36 lookbook photos · checked November 19, 2026
The TGI Take

In Vol. 1 we went to South Africa, Portugal, Japan, Sweden and Canada. The idea holds: independent golf is community first. A brand from another country is a local’s view of the game there, its weather, its courses, its jokes, and the best way in before you book a flight.

How we picked. One brand per country, six new countries, each brand from our own coverage and each one that could only have come from where it is. Hiroki brings Japanese precision to Auckland. Fyfe cuts Harris Tweed in Fife. SLICE TOWN dresses Copenhagen’s simulator crowd. Rebolf turns Barcelona football kits into golf clothes. Dim Sum Golf Club is a Hong Kong club first and a brand second. Gray + Haast dresses the Gold Coast. Every piece here was in stock on November 19, 2026.

Then we went to their backyard. For each country, the most dramatic course a visitor can actually book: cliff-top fairways 140 meters above the Pacific, a Fife links where every hole meets the sea, a royal deer park outside Copenhagen, the 2031 Ryder Cup venue, an island you reach by ferry and a lighthouse links in Bass Strait.

A note on prices. We show each price the way the store shows it to a US visitor. Five of the six stores show US dollars. Dim Sum Golf Club prices in Hong Kong dollars, so those dollar figures are approximate. Hiroki and Gray + Haast were running sales, and we show the original price beside the sale price.

What to buy first. The Fyfe Carlogie mallet cover. Harris Tweed from the Outer Hebrides and a gold Lion Rampant could only come from Scotland. On a budget, Dim Sum’s ball marker and sock set is about $23.

Where to go first. Cape Kidnappers. There is nothing else in golf like hitting an approach along a ridge with a 140-meter drop on both sides. If the flight is too far, Kingsbarns is seven miles from St Andrews.

01 · New Zealand

Hiroki

Auckland

Hiroki is an Auckland brand with a Japanese-sounding name, and the name is deliberate. The brand says it was chosen to evoke the precision of Japanese craftsmanship and to work as a filter: if a design feels forced, it does not belong.

The range is narrow on purpose: stand bags, leather Sunday bags, a handful of headcovers and some hats, with the bags named for places from Tokyo to Wellington. The two hats here, made with Edds, carry the names of New Zealand clubs, Apiti and Hutt Park. Hiroki also runs its own events, like a 25-team Summer Shootout at Jack’s Point. Every piece here was on sale when we checked; the original prices are shown.

Where to play in New ZealandCape Kidnappers · Hawke’s Bay, North Island

Cape Kidnappers

Cape Kidnappers is the most dramatic round in New Zealand. Tom Doak laid it out across a peninsula at the southern end of Hawke’s Bay, on a working sheep and cattle station, and the fairways run out along ridges that stop at cliffs about 140 meters above the Pacific. Doak’s own line on the site: if it were any more dramatic, “it would probably be cordoned off as a national park.”

It opened in 2004. Three times you carry from the end of one ridge to the next, and at the 6th and 15th a pulled approach can leave the planet: Doak reckons the ball hangs for nearly ten seconds before it reaches the ocean. The land around it is a predator-fenced wildlife sanctuary, so expect rare birds in the ravines.

Where
Hawke’s Bay, on the North Island’s east coast
Green fee
NZ$975 (~$550) international, NZ$500 (~$282) for NZ residents, Oct 2026–Apr 2027
Good to know
Lodge guests get 10% off; caddies available
02 · Scotland

Fyfe

Fife

Fyfe makes headcovers and bags by hand in Scotland. Neil Rennie founded it in Fife in 2021, and the materials are as Scottish as they come: Harris Tweed woven in the Outer Hebrides, tartans, and waxed canvas made for links weather.

Fyfe’s best covers are the colorful ones, so that is what we picked: a racing-red leather blade, a green-and-yellow Fescue, Bay Hill with its bright orange lining, a rust-and-teal Jura Sunset check and a green Carlogie mallet with a gold Lion Rampant. The store shows US visitors dollar prices.

Where to play in ScotlandKingsbarns Golf Links · Kingsbarns, Fife

Kingsbarns Golf Links

Fyfe was founded in Fife, so the backyard course had to be in the Kingdom. Kingsbarns sits seven miles from St Andrews along almost two miles of North Sea coast, and the club’s own pitch is that every hole embraces the sea. It looks like it has been there for centuries. It opened in 2000, a Kyle Phillips design, though golf on the site goes back to an 18th-century golfing society.

It is one of the three courses that host the Alfred Dunhill Links every October, alongside the Old Course and Carnoustie. Fairways roll through gorse and dune right down to the rocks. It is not cheap and it closes for winter, so plan it for summer.

Where
Seven miles southeast of St Andrews
Green fee
£486 (~$646), May–early November 2026
Good to know
Closed in winter; book well ahead
03 · Denmark

SLICE TOWN

Copenhagen

SLICE TOWN makes golf clothes for people who came to the game through simulators and late-night range sessions. A Dane and a Swede started it at the end of 2024, and the clothes are designed in Copenhagen. Its line on its own About page: “Our purpose is to challenge tradition, and make it possible for golfers to wear whatever they want on the course.”

It puts as much into events as product, including a late-night simulator tournament in Copenhagen with a DJ and a bar. The pieces here are the easy way in: three caps, a box of socks and the issue of Depeche Golf that put SLICE TOWN in its game changers section. The store shows US visitors dollar prices.

Where to play in DenmarkKøbenhavns Golf Klub · Dyrehaven, Klampenborg

Københavns Golf Klub

Københavns Golf Klub is the first golf club in Scandinavia, founded in 1898. Its first members played on a common in Copenhagen and dug the holes themselves every morning, then filled them in again at night. In 1899 it moved to Dyrehaven, the royal deer park north of the city, and the 18-hole course it opened there in 1928 is still largely the one you play today.

The deer never left. The course runs across the open plain below the old royal hunting lodge, the Hermitage, among herds of fallow and red deer, and the club plays summer greens year-round. It is part golf course, part safari, a short train ride from the city.

Where
Klampenborg, north of central Copenhagen
Green fee
Confirm with the club; a registered handicap is required
Good to know
Deer on the course, summer greens year-round
04 · Spain

Rebolf

Barcelona

Rebolf is from Barcelona, founded by a golf professional who, in the brand’s words, “felt he needed a change.” Its clothes are made at a local studio in small runs, and they look more like a football kit than a pro shop. Rebolf puts it this way: “We bridge the gap between the fairway and the street.”

The newest collection goes to the mountains: a patched full-zip and a Winter Golf Games long-sleeve polo, shot in the snow. We paired them with a yellow football polo, a teal tech polo, pleated Riviera shorts and the croc belt Rebolf makes with Balteus. The store shows US visitors dollar prices.

Where to play in SpainCamiral, Stadium Course · Caldes de Malavella, Girona

Camiral, Stadium Course

Camiral, the resort long known as PGA Catalunya, sits in the pine woods near Girona, about an hour from Barcelona. Its Stadium Course, by Neil Coles and Ángel Gallardo, has been testing tour players since 1999 and has hosted the Spanish Open three times. Lakes wrap the greens, umbrella pines line every fairway, and the course is irrigated entirely with recycled water.

In 2031 it hosts the Ryder Cup, the first in Spain in more than 30 years. Play it now, before everyone else wants to. Green fees include a buggy, and men need a handicap of 28 or lower.

Where
Near Girona, about 1 hour from Barcelona
Green fee
€143–€192 with buggy (~$157–$211)
Good to know

05 · Hong Kong

Dim Sum Golf Club

Hong Kong

Dim Sum Golf Club started in Hong Kong in the winter of 2023. Its founder, Luke, a UK expat, ran into how limited and exclusive golf access is in the city, so he started a club for people who just wanted to play more. Joining is free; a paid membership adds golf days, simulator nights and trips around Asia.

The clothes come second to the club, and they are full of inside jokes for people who know the city: a dumpling-basket crest, a HoleInWan cap, a ball marker shaped like a steamer. Prices are in Hong Kong dollars; the dollar figures here are approximate. The store notes there are no refunds, and exchanges are only in Hong Kong.

Where to play in Hong KongKau Sai Chau · Sai Kung

Kau Sai Chau

Kau Sai Chau is Hong Kong’s only public golf course, and the trip is half the point: you take a ferry from Sai Kung Town to an island with three courses on it and the mountains of the New Territories all around. The North and South Courses are Gary Player designs; the East Course is by Nelson & Haworth.

It is the course Dim Sum’s Kau Sai Chau T-shirt is drawn from, and it is the one round in Hong Kong a visitor can book without knowing a member. Book ahead, and bring an appetite: Sai Kung’s seafood restaurants are waiting at the pier when you get back.

Where
Kau Sai Chau island, by ferry from Sai Kung
Green fee
About HK$1,030–1,360 (~$132–$174) for visitors at the last published rate; confirm with the club
Good to know
The only public golf in Hong Kong

Where to play in AustraliaCape Wickham Links · King Island, Tasmania

Cape Wickham Links

Cape Wickham is golf at the end of the map. It sits on the northern tip of King Island, out in Bass Strait between Tasmania and the mainland, and it is routed around the Cape Wickham Lighthouse, built in 1861. Mike DeVries and Darius Oliver designed it, and it opened to the public in late 2015.

Holes run along white-sand beaches and rocky headlands, and the sea is in view the whole way round. Walk it with a caddie if you can; the greens are fescue, and the wind off the strait decides everything. Stay on site and the all-day fee costs the same as 18 holes.

Where
King Island, in Bass Strait, Tasmania
Green fee
A$315 (~$205) for international visitors, A$235 (~$153) for Australians, 2026
Good to know
Book by phone or email; caddies two weeks ahead
