Practice With a Purpose: An Off-Season Plan for Deliberate, Thoughtful Golf Practice

By Lenny Harrington · Updated November 8, 2026

Field Notes · The off-season practice plan3 sessions, 12 weeks, 8 tools · checked November 8, 2026
The TGI Take

Most range sessions look the same: a big bucket, the 7-iron that felt good last week, swing until it is empty. It feels productive. It mostly isn’t.

The off season is the time to fix that. No match on Saturday, nothing to lose by trying something that feels worse before it feels better. The plan is simple: practice less, practice harder. One goal per session. Honest feedback. Never the same shot twice. Same test every week, written down.

01 · The idea

What Deliberate Practice Is

The term comes from psychologist Anders Ericsson, whose work was later squeezed into the “10,000-hour rule.” He spent years correcting that. Hours were never the point; the kind of practice was.

A specific goal (“start 8 of 10 four-footers on line,” not “putt better”), full focus, honest feedback, and drills just past what you can already do. Hitting balls with a vague hope of improving is what researchers call naive practice.

The loop, every session
02 · The research

Mix It Up, Even Though It Feels Worse

Blocked practice repeats one shot. Random practice changes it every swing. Blocked looks better on the day; random is what sticks.

In a 2017 trial with 30 new golfers, the random group putted worse in practice but better on the retention test. A 2018 study found the same for chipping. The studies are small and mostly beginners, but on the course you never hit the same shot twice, so practice that way. If it feels a little frustrating, it is probably working.

03 · Attention

Think Target, Not Elbow

Gabriele Wulf’s studies found golfers hit more accurate pitches focusing on the club or the target than on their arms, beginners and experts alike. Body cues still have a place: use them to learn a new position at home, then put your attention back on the target when you play.

Internal“Keep my left arm straight.”“Turn my hips.”Fine for learning a position at home
External“Swing the clubhead at the flag.”“Start it over that tree.”More accurate on the range and the course
04 · The sessions

Three Sessions a Week

Two at home, one at the range, one round of practice golf. Each starts with one written goal and ends with a number.

Home · 20 min

The putting block

  1. Start line. Two tees just wider than the ball, a foot ahead of it. Roll 4-footers through the gate.
  2. Ladder. 3, 6 and 9 feet in random order. A point per make.
  3. Test. Ten 4-footers from different spots. Write the number down.
Range · 45 min

No two shots alike

  1. Warm up. Ten wedges, stick on your target line.
  2. Play a hole. Driver, the approach you would leave, a wedge. New club and target every swing.
  3. Pressure. Ten shots at a target; three misses and you start over.
Course · 9 holes

Practice golf

  1. Go late, no card.
  2. Score targets. A point each for fairway, green or fringe, and up and down.
  3. Worst ball inside 100. Hit two, play the worse. The costliest shot is next week’s goal.
05 · Feedback

Keep Score on Your Practice

Every session gets a date, one goal and a test score. Keep the tests the same week to week so the numbers mean something. Change one thing at a time, and expect scores to dip before they climb.

Read and Watch

For the nights it is too cold or too dark. Book links go to Bookshop.org, which supports independent bookstores. For the classics, see our seven books to elevate your game.

Books

Peak by Anders Ericsson and Robert Pool. The source: what deliberate practice is, from the researcher who named it.
The Practice Manual by Adam Young. Motor-learning research turned into golf practice you can actually run.
Every Shot Counts by Mark Broadie. The strokes-gained book. Shows where your shots are really lost, so you know what to practice.
The Talent Code by Daniel Coyle. Why slow, focused, mistake-filled practice builds skill, across sports and music.

YouTube

Adam Young Golf. The Practice Manual author on strike, variability and how people learn.
Athletic Motion Golf. Clear drills, many needing nothing more than an alignment stick.
Me and My Golf. PGA coaches Piers Ward and Andy Proudman; drills for every level.

The Last Word

Less, but Better

The golfers who come out of winter better rarely hit the most balls. They knew what they were working on, got honest feedback and kept score. Start with twenty minutes on the living room floor, twice a week, and a number written down at the end.
