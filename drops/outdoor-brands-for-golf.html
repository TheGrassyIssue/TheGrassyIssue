If It Can Survive the Mountain, It Can Survive the Muni

By Lenny Harrington · Updated October 22, 2026

The Grassy Issue · Outdoor brands for golf35 picks · prices checked October 22, 2026
The TGI Take

We like our golf gear the way mountain people like theirs: durable, stain resistant, weather resistant, and still going next winter, not just this one. The thinking is simple. If it can survive the mountain, it will be fine at the local muni, especially in January.

January is when it matters. An Austin winter round is a cold, windy first tee, fairways soaked with dew, maybe a drizzle, and an afternoon that can turn warm by the back nine. That is a layering problem, and outdoor brands have been solving layering problems for a long time. Golf brands mostly make polos.

What we looked for. Stretch or gussets so the turn is free; wind blocking and water repellency that stays quiet at address; fabric that shrugs off grass stains and mud; pockets that zip; and shoes that keep socks dry on wet grass. We skipped harnesses, ski kit and heavy down, and anything that looks like you got lost on the way to the summit.

Five brands. Arc’teryx, started by Vancouver climbers in 1989. Salomon, from the French Alps since 1947. Gramicci, a Yosemite climber’s pant brand from 1982. Purple Mountain Observatory, a London label from 2021. And Goldwin, a Japanese ski maker from 1950 that has made golf clothes since 1981, which makes it the bridge between the two worlds.

If your winters run milder, our Technical Brands Roundup, Vol. 2 covers wind, light rain and muggy afternoons.

A note on shoes: trail and hiking soles grip wet grass well, but they aren’t golf soles. Check your course’s footwear rules before you walk one onto a green.

Below: seven pieces from each brand, 35 in all, from a $50 cap to a $385 rain jacket, and one January kit built from them. Everything was in stock on each brand’s US store when we checked on October 22, 2026.

Arc’teryx · North Vancouver, 1989. Started by Vancouver climbers in a basement making harnesses and packs, named for the Archaeopteryx fossil, and still designing and testing in the Coast Mountains of British Columbia. Gore-Tex apparel came in 1996; its ReBIRD program repairs and resells used gear.

“Every piece we make serves a purpose and solves a problem for mountain athletes, delivering functionality, simplicity, and durability in a timeless design.”— Arc’teryx

The Questions

Can you wear hiking or trail shoes to play golf?

Many public courses allow soft-soled athletic shoes, but rules vary and some ask for golf-specific soles. Low trail shoes with Gore-Tex keep your feet dry on wet grass; check the course's footwear policy before you play.

What should I wear to play golf in cold weather?

Layers: a thin quarter-zip or fleece, an insulated vest that leaves the arms free, a packable wind or rain shell, wind-blocking pants and waterproof shoes. Shed layers as the day warms up.

Is Arc'teryx good for golf?

Several Arc'teryx pieces work well on a course, especially the stretch Gamma Pant, the Atom Vest, Kyanite and Delta fleeces and the packable Squamish windshell. They are built for movement and weather.

What are the best waterproof shoes for winter golf?

If your course allows soft-soled athletic shoes, low Gore-Tex trail shoes such as the Salomon XT-6 GORE-TEX, XA PRO 3D V9 GORE-TEX or Arc'teryx Kopec 2 Low GTX keep feet dry on wet morning grass. Check the course's footwear rules first.

What pants are good for golf in cold weather?

Look for stretch, a gusseted crotch and wind or water resistance. Good options include the Arc'teryx Gamma Pant, Goldwin Nimble Tech Tapered Pants, the Gramicci Nylon Gramicci Pant and the Purple Mountain Observatory City Pant.

Are Gramicci pants good for golf?

Yes. Gramicci pants were built for climbing, with a gusset for movement, and the nylon versions are water-repellent and dry fast, which suits wet fairways. The built-in waistband adjusts quickly mid-round.

Does Goldwin make golf clothes?

Yes. Goldwin, the Japanese outdoor and ski-wear company, has made golf apparel since a U.S. Open Golf line in 1981 and was an official supplier to the U.S. Open from 1997. Its current US line is outdoor and lifestyle clothing.
