The White Tee Edit — 17 From the Universe and Beyond

Drops & Brands · 29 August 2026 · 17 pieces · $35–$102

A white golf tee is the least forgiving thing you can put on. There is no pattern hiding the cut, no colour flattering the cloth, and every wash shows. Which is exactly why it rewards doing properly: the good ones are obvious and so are the bad ones.

The reference here is less golf than it is the plain American tee — heavyweight jersey, boxy through the body, a graphic that either earns its place or is not there at all. What has changed is who is making them. Five of the seventeen below come from labels that have never appeared on this site, and two of those are better than anything the established names shipped this year.

Who it is for: the golfer who wants one shirt that works for the range, the round and the drive home, and would rather it not announce a brand from forty yards. Seventeen tees, one per label, every price and size checked against the brand’s own store this week. They run from thirty-five euro to a hundred and two dollars, and the cheapest one is not the worst one.

Two things to know before you click. Radry makes exactly one pale tee and it is down to two sizes, so that one is a race. And Metalwood is the only label here with real depth in white, which is why it is the single card carrying two shirts rather than two cards carrying one each.

What should I look for in a white golf tee shirt?

Opacity first — a thin white tee goes translucent in Texas sun and under a wet back. Then fabric: mercerised or Supima cotton holds its shape and resists the greying that kills cheap white tees after ten washes; a cotton-poly or Tencel blend dries faster if you sweat heavily. Check the collar construction too, because a soft ribbed collar is what separates a tee you can wear to dinner from one that looks slept in by the ninth.

Are white tee shirts allowed on golf courses?

At most public and resort courses, yes — a plain, clean, well-fitting tee is fine. Private clubs are the exception and many still require a collar, so check before you play. The safe read is that the tighter the dress code, the more a tee needs to look deliberate rather than casual: heavier fabric, clean hem, no slogans.

How do I stop a white tee going yellow or grey?

Wash cold and separately from anything coloured, skip the fabric softener, which coats fibres and traps residue, and dry on low or hang it. Most yellowing at the collar and underarms is antiperspirant reacting with sweat rather than dirt, so rinse those areas before they go in the machine. Chlorine bleach makes it worse over time — oxygen bleach is the safer option.

What is the difference between mercerised and regular cotton?

Mercerising treats the yarn under tension in a caustic solution, which swells the fibre, makes it stronger, more lustrous and better at holding dye. In a white tee the practical result is a smoother, slightly cooler-feeling fabric that keeps its shape and resists pilling. It costs more, and it is most of what you are paying for in a $60 tee versus a $20 one.

Which brands make the best plain white golf tees?

This edit covers seventeen from across the independent golf world, from Criquet in Austin and Huega House in California to ANTi Country Club Tokyo, Odd Ritual in Cape Town, Casualist in London and Rebolf in Barcelona. Prices run from roughly $35 to $80. What separates them is fabric weight and collar construction rather than logo.

Is a white tee too casual to wear to the clubhouse?

Not if the fabric carries it. A heavyweight tee in mercerised or Supima cotton, cut to actually fit, reads as intentional; a thin promotional tee does not. The tee shirts in this edit were chosen partly on that test — whether the piece still looks right when you sit down for a drink after the round.
