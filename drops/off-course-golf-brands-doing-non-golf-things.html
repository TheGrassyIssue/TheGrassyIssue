Off Course — Golf Brands Doing Non-Golf Things

18 Brands Coffee to Comic Books The Culture Extends

Golf brands used to stay in their lane — polos, hats, bags, repeat. Not anymore. The most interesting names in the game are branching into everything from specialty coffee to children’s books, handcrafted candles to honey habanero hot sauce. What started as novelty collaborations has become a full cultural movement. Golf brands don’t just make gear now — they roast beans, press vinyl, pour wax, and bottle sauce.

This isn’t about golfers slapping their names on products. It’s about brands born in golf culture deciding that culture doesn’t end at the 18th green. Gumtree Golf roasts coffee in the same Brooklyn studio where they sew headcovers from vintage fabrics. Good Lion Golf designed a hot sauce specifically to survive a round in your bag without refrigeration. Muni Kids publishes actual children’s books and sells hand-painted fine art alongside their streetwear drops. The throughline isn’t golf — it’s taste.

Here are 18 brands doing it right.

Brooklyn-roasted from the same studio where they sew headcovers from vintage fabrics. Notes of chocolate cream, orange zest, almond cookies. Sustainably sourced, beyond Fair-Trade wages. Monthly subscription box includes coffee, Callaway balls, and tees.

Non-Golf Kicks
Whim Golf × Reebok
Classic Leather · $130
Chicago golf brand dropped a Reebok Classic Leather in blue suede — explicitly “not a golf shoe.” Sold at Hypebeast, END., and Packer Shoes. Also makes Vibram dial slippers and installs free pop-up putting greens across major cities.

Eastside Golf × Jordan Brand
Air Jordan 1 Low G
Nine Jordan collabs and counting. The Air Jordan 4 “Be You” resells for $15,000. Morehouse College golf teammates Olajuwon Ajanaku and Earl Cooper building the biggest cultural crossover in golf. Mercedes-Benz partnership, airport retail stores, Hulu docuseries.

Food
Good Lion Golf
Mantra Sauce · Honey Habanero
Golf apparel brand that made a honey habanero hot sauce designed to live in your golf bag. Collab with Mantra Sauce. No refrigeration needed — shelf-stable for on-course use. The most specific product-market fit on this list.

Kingmade Jerky
Official Jerky of the PGA Tour
Founded by PGA Tour caddie Jeff King, who built a jerky brand from inside the ropes. Premium grass-fed flank steak, carried by pros during rounds. From the loop to the pro shop — the only caddie-founded food brand on Tour.

Quiet Golf

The marshal’s “Quiet Please” sign reimagined as a 10-inch acrylic incense holder. Also makes a Ceramic Owl Crest Tray ($70) that sells out repeatedly. Listed on SSENSE alongside Comme des Garçons and Acne Studios. Golf as design object.

Brooklyn niche perfume house made a $210 cologne described as “a golf fantasy by a guy who doesn’t golf.” Notes of lime, leather, grass, moss, and cedar — inspired by 1980s New England club locker rooms. Also sold at Todd Snyder.

Golf performance brand expanding into a full grooming suite — shaving cream, moisturizer, deodorant, body wash. Next flagship store (6,000 sq ft) will include a coffee shop, barbershop, and lounge. A golf brand with its own barber chair.

Wild Cards
Muni Kids
Golforia Children’s Book
Portland golf brand publishing “Golforia” — a children’s book series teaching golf etiquette through illustrated adventure. Also sells hand-painted acrylic fine art (up to $2,000), comic books, squirt guns, cue balls, and hip-hop tribute collections. The widest product range of any golf brand, period.
Shop ↗
Linksoul
Lab + Goat Hill Park
John Ashworth’s surf-golf brand saved Goat Hill Park — a 1952-era municipal course — from demolition, securing a 30-year lease. Runs Linksoul Lab in Oceanside: part retail, part art gallery, part community space with ping-pong, concerts, and film screenings. “World class, working class.” Green fee: $26.

Ernie Els Pet Products
Champion Training Treats
Four-time major champion running a legitimate pet brand — not merch, a real pet company. Human-grade chicken and salmon training treats, golf-themed collars, sold on Chewy and Amazon. 10% of profits to the Els for Autism Foundation.
