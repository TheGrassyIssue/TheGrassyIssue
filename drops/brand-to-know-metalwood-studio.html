Metalwood Studio — Brand to Know

metalwood.studio Los Angeles, CA Updated Aug 2026

Metalwood Studio is what happens when skate graphics and indie brand culture crash into golf — and nobody apologizes for it. Based on medieval heraldry and industrial signage, the design language is unlike anything else in the space. The Tri-Metal Tee at $54 is the entry point. The Bomber Jacket at $228 and Mechanic Jacket at $278 are the statement pieces.

The Medieval Terry Polo, the World Class Zip Polo, the Madras Explorer Shirt — every piece looks like it was designed by someone who cares more about art direction than sell-through. That's the whole point, and it's working.

And the collab résumé is unmatched for a brand this size. Before adidas came calling, Metalwood had already put its name on a FootJoy golf sneaker, a KEEN footwear capsule, a Maxfli collection that leaned all the way into the golden-era nostalgia, a Garrett Leight eyewear collab, an exclusive capsule with American Express and Hypebeast, and — most recently — the Oakley Pro M Frame, the '90s tour staple reissued through Metalwood's lens in a friends-and-family run that had collectors refreshing their feeds. Almost none of it can be bought today, which is exactly the point.

Revisited, August 2026: the story got bigger. Founder Cole Young — Carlsbad kid, D1 golfer at Loyola Marymount, four years inside Malbon Golf before betting on himself — turned an Instagram moodboard of '90s golfers in pleated pants into a La Brea Avenue studio with a global cult following. 2026 is the adidas year: the MC70 golf shoe with deco-stitching pulled from 1970s football boots, and a Predator Sala sneaker rebuilt in shaggy suede that had a line down the block in January. Below: the current collection's best, the new-era collab pieces, and Metalwood in the wild — from the LA store to a Paris party with Oakley.

The Story — FAQ
What is Metalwood Studio?
A Los Angeles golf and lifestyle brand built as an ode to golf in the 1990s and early 2000s — the era when persimmon gave way to metal woods, sleeves ran to the elbow, and pleats were everywhere. Founder Cole Young calls the lane it occupies “a subculture of a subculture of a subculture”: high-end yet unassuming, vintage yet never been done, rooted in golf yet often about everything around it.
Who founded Metalwood Studio?
Cole Young — a Carlsbad, California kid who learned the game on sawed-off Ben Hogan Redlines his grandfather cut down for him, played D1 golf at Loyola Marymount, then spent nearly four years in wholesaling and marketing at Malbon Golf. Metalwood started as an Instagram moodboard of '90s golfers in baggy pleated pants while he was still at Malbon. It grew through the pandemic, he went fully independent — “I was done helping people build their own empires” — and by early 2022 had a studio on La Brea Avenue in Los Angeles.
What does the name Metalwood mean?
A metalwood is the club that killed persimmon — the steel-headed drivers and fairway woods that took over golf in the late '80s and '90s. Naming the brand after that transition is the whole thesis: the gear, the graphics, and the attitude of golf's most underrated design decade, treated like it matters. Blades and bargain-bin clubs are worshipped; Fred Couples is a demigod.
What is the Metalwood x adidas collab?
The 2026 partnership, first teased during Paris Fashion Week: the adidas x Metalwood MC70 golf shoe ($200) with deco-stitched leather pulled from 1970s football boots, plus a polo, windbreaker, pant, hat, and glove — and the Metalwood x adidas Skateboarding Predator Sala ($150), the indoor-soccer icon rebuilt in shaggy suede, which launched at the LA store in January 2026 before hitting the CONFIRMED app.
Is Metalwood a streetwear brand?
Cole Young resists the label. In his words, streetwear got ruined by “sneakerheadism” — people treating scarce purchases as a personality. Metalwood's counterweight is self-deprecation: the brand roasts itself in its own product copy, and the site's meta description literally reads “tHiS uSeD tO bE a gOLf bRaNd :/”. Anti-pretentious is the identity.
Who has Metalwood collaborated with?
adidas (golf and skateboarding), Garrett Leight, the BMW Championship, Jones Golf Bags, Skratch TV, and Imperial headwear — plus events beyond product, like a group photo show at the La Brea store and a Metalwood & Oakley night in Paris. TGI has covered the MC70 in our Golf Shoe Edit and their crew sock in the Sock Drawer.
What collabs has Metalwood Studio done?
Beyond the current adidas partnership: a FootJoy golf sneaker, a KEEN footwear capsule, a Maxfli golden-era collection, Garrett Leight eyewear, an exclusive American Express x Hypebeast capsule, and the 2025 Oakley Pro M Frame reissue. Nearly all of it sold out and now trades hands on the secondary market.
