Field Note: Drawing It Up, a Golf Trip to London

Field Note London, Surrey and Berkshire 5 days · 5 rounds · checked November 17, 2026
Royal Blackheath at sunset, with the City and Canary Wharf on the horizon. Photo: Royal Blackheath Golf Club

“This is the epitome of golf, on open heathland, under a big sky.”— Walton Heath Golf Club

This one started with an art show. Beyond the Streets is back at the Saatchi Gallery until May 5, 2027, and once I saw it I started drawing up a trip around it. Nothing is booked yet. This is the plan on paper: five days in London with old golf courses, good art, interesting stores and long dinners.

The golf is the reason to go. London is ringed by some of the oldest and best heathland golf in the world, and most of it takes visitors if you know how to ask. We would play the oldest club in England with the City on the horizon, give a full day to Walton Heath and Swinley Forest, play Abercromby’s course at The Addington, and finish in a red shirt on Wimbledon Common, where the rules say you have to wear one.

We are thinking late April 2027, Monday to Friday, before the show closes. Swinley only takes visitors on weekdays, Borough Market is closed Mondays and Royal Blackheath is weekdays only, which is how the days fell into this order. Below is every stop, what it costs, how to book it and why it is on the list.

Day One · Chelsea, Soho and St James’s

Art and Shopping

Land, drop the bags and go straight to the show the trip is planned around. Then Palace’s new flagship and Dover Street Market, and dinner at a counter on Haymarket.

Beyond the Streets

Saatchi Gallery · King’s Road, Chelsea

This is the stop the whole trip is built around. Roger Gastman’s Beyond the Streets has come back to the Saatchi Gallery with an all-new show: more than 100 artists across more than ten rooms of the Duke of York’s HQ, tracing six decades of graffiti, hip-hop, 2Tone, fashion and protest.

The details are what make it. Part of a New York subway car has been rebuilt inside the gallery. There is a history of graffiti on the London Underground, a spray-paint infinity room, early Polaroids of hip-hop stars from 1971, an archive of English football firms’ calling cards and 35 covers from The Big Issue. On the walls: KAWS, FUTURA 2000, LADY PINK, Kenny Scharf, Jenny Holzer, Shepard Fairey and Goldie.

Gastman’s first London show here in 2023 drew more than 150,000 people in three months, so we will book a morning slot and give it a few hours. The gallery shop is selling a 500-page catalogue for the show, which would be the one souvenir I’d carry home.

Late afternoonSoho to Haymarket

Palace and Dover Street Market

8 Poland Street, Soho · 18–22 Haymarket, St James’s

From Chelsea, east to Soho for the two best stores in town. Palace closed its original Brewer Street shop in September and opened a new London flagship around the corner on Poland Street on November 5, 2026. The press around the opening describes a store built from London references, with stained glass and raw oak seating, and it launched with a London-only Burberry collaboration and a run of pieces carrying the number eight for the address.

Then a 10-minute walk south to Dover Street Market. Comme des Garçons’ store left Dover Street for a bigger building on Haymarket in 2016 and kept the old name. It is less a shop than a rotating exhibition of the labels it rates, and Palace has its own space inside. Go in through the Orange Street entrance and work your way up.

Between the two you see where the clothes golf keeps borrowing from are going next. Dinner is a few doors down.

England’s Oldest Club

Royal Blackheath Golf Club

Eltham, southeast London · Thomas Dunn · 1892

The club’s story starts with a Scottish king. When James VI of Scotland became James I of England in 1603 and moved his court to Greenwich, his courtiers brought golf with them and played on the high ground at Blackheath. The club says it was “instituted” in 1608, and is honest that there is no paper to prove the date, because its accounts before 1787 are missing. What it can say for certain is that it is the oldest golf club in England.

The clubhouse is Eltham Lodge, a Grade I house built in 1664 for the banker to Charles I. The club moved here from the heath in 1923, onto a course Thomas Dunn laid out in 1892 and James Braid later added to. Inside is a museum with the club’s trophies, including the silver claret jug Blackheath won at St Andrews in 1857, the first time a claret jug was used as a golf trophy. The Spring Medal, first played here in 1792, is the oldest golfing medal in the world.

The club’s own aerial photo, the one that opens this post, has the City and Canary Wharf on the horizon. It is the most London round of the week, and at £80 the best value.

LunchLondon Bridge

Borough Market

8 Southwark Street · London Bridge

The train back from Eltham comes into London Bridge, and Borough Market is right outside the station. The sign over the entrance says established 1756, and it is free to walk in. Lunch is whatever looks best on the day.

The market is in three parts: Three Crown Square for the bigger producers and merchants, the Green Market for small specialist traders and Borough Market Kitchen for street food. Come hungry and do a lap before buying anything. It is closed on Mondays, which is part of why this is a Tuesday.

AfternoonWaterloo

Leake Street Arches

Beneath Waterloo Station

After yesterday’s gallery version, the real thing. A walk west along the river ends at the tunnel under Waterloo station, home to what the arches call London’s longest legal graffiti wall. It started with a festival Banksy organised in 2008, and nothing on the walls lasts long.

Anyone can bring a can and paint, and the official artists in residence run graffiti workshops. The arches also hold bars and restaurants: Passyunk Avenue, an American dive bar, and Draughts, a board-game café with more than 1,000 games. An afternoon beer underground before dinner is the plan.

DinnerSmithfield

St. JOHN Smithfield

26 St John Street · Clerkenwell

St. JOHN opened in 1994 in a derelict Georgian smokehouse beside Smithfield Meat Market, and to us it is still the reference point for eating in London. The idea was nose-to-tail cooking: every cut, the innards and the extremities that usually get thrown away, cooked simply and served in a plain white room.

The menu is still written twice a day, every day, and the bread is baked each morning. Their own words for the place are “simplicity, seasonality, and the whole beast.” This is the dinner we would book first.

Walton Heath, the Old Course

Walton on the Hill, Surrey · Herbert Fowler · 1903

The morning round is Walton Heath, and the history here is the best of the trip. The club was founded in 1903. The club lists King Edward VIII as its first captain, its first professional was James Braid, who won five Opens and stayed for some forty-five years, and Winston Churchill played here regularly. It hosted the Ryder Cup in 1981.

Herbert Fowler designed the Old Course, and the club says it has been in the world’s top 100 every year since the rankings began in 1938. It is open heathland under a big sky, longer than most of its neighbors with generally smaller greens, so it asks for what the club calls “Scottish shots.” Jack Nicklaus described the two courses as “wonderfully pure.”

There is no halfway hut, so we will buy water before the 1st. Caddies are £80 plus tip, paid in cash on the day, and the club has no cash machine. No handicap certificate is needed, only that visitors are competent golfers.

Swinley Forest Golf Club

Ascot, Berkshire · Harry Colt

After lunch, west into Berkshire for the quieter round of the day. Harry Colt designed Swinley through pine and heather, and the club has stayed small and private. On weekdays it welcomes a limited number of visitors “strictly by prior arrangement,” so this is an email to the club office, not a booking page.

The house rules are part of the experience. Tailored shorts are fine, but no collarless shirts, jeans or trainers. Phones stay in the car park or the changing room. Jackets are required in the upstairs dining room, where the club serves a carvery that looks out over the course. If we get the afternoon, it will be the most peaceful round of the trip.

35 Maiden Lane · Covent Garden

Back into town for the oldest restaurant in London. Thomas Rule opened it on Maiden Lane in 1798, and in more than two centuries it has had just four owning families. The cooking is traditional British with a focus on game.

After 36 holes of heathland, this is the right kind of dinner: old paintings on the walls, white tablecloths and a cocktail bar for after.

The Addington

Croydon, Surrey · J.F. Abercromby and Harry Colt · 1913

The closest of the great heathland courses to central London, and the one with a cult following. John Frederick Abercromby designed it with Harry Colt in 1913: pine, heather, rolling ground, light bunkering and a par of 69, with the city skyline in the background on many holes.

Since 2020 the club has been restoring the course to how it looked originally, and the recent drone photography shows it. It is less famous than its Surrey neighbors, which is part of the appeal. Visitors book online every day of the week.

Bring a camera. With the morning light coming through the pines and London on the horizon, it might give us the best photos of the trip.

AfternoonBankside

Tate Modern

Bankside · Monet and Julio Le Parc

After the Addington, back up to Bankside. Tate Modern is free to walk into, and in late April it has two shows to plan around, from opposite ends of art history.

Monet: Painting Time is the gallery’s first exhibition devoted only to Claude Monet, with loans from around the world, including rarely seen works from private collections. It follows how Monet answered railways, the modern city and standardized time, from Arrival of the Normandy Train, Gare Saint-Lazare (1877) to the Poplars series and the Water Lilies he painted at Giverny.

Then the opposite: the Julio Le Parc show, the Argentine artist’s rooms of moving light, mirrors and color, on until May 3. After a morning in the heather, it is the most fun hour of the trip.

The Quality Chop House

94 Farringdon Road · Clerkenwell

A chop house since 1869, which turned 150 in 2019. The current owners say they are proud of the site’s “meaty” past but don’t lean on it, and the menu is modern British built on relationships with the best suppliers in the country.

It is close to St. JOHN, and the two make a good pair of bookends to the week. Nathan Chapman runs the kitchen. Dinner is served until 9:45pm. Order the chop.

Wimbledon Common Golf Club

19 Camp Road, Wimbledon · Founded 1908

The last round, and the most fun on paper. Wimbledon Common is a woodland course eight miles from central London that runs across public common land, shared with walkers, dogs and a second club, London Scottish. Because of that there is a local rule: every golfer wears a plain pillar-box red top so everyone else can see you coming.

Forget yours and the club will sell or hire you one. No handicap certificate is needed, you just have to know the rules and etiquette. Denim is banned. Tee times open five days ahead online, and you pay when you arrive. The course closes at 2pm on Sundays so the public can use the common.

At £35 (~$47) it is the cheapest round of the week, and the best place to play with friends. If anyone from the list below joins us, this is the round.

AfternoonCanary Wharf

TrendyGolf at Pitch Wharf

30 Churchill Place · Canary Wharf

TrendyGolf has sold golf clothes online since 2004, and in 2025 it opened its first UK concept store inside Pitch Wharf. The store is 2,300 square feet. Malbon, Manors, G/FORE, J.Lindeberg and KJUS are on the rails, and FootJoy, Nike and Puma are on the shoe wall. Several of those brands are hard to find in person in the UK.

Pitch Wharf is Pitch’s flagship and its largest London venue: 11 Trackman bays, a bar and kitchen and a terrace on the water. Shop first, then book a bay and settle the week’s bets on a simulator before dinner.

Last nightCovent Garden

Dishoom Covent Garden

12 Upper St Martin’s Lane · Covent Garden

The last night. Dishoom’s Covent Garden café is built around 1949 Bombay and its picture houses, with old film posters and photographs on every wall. It is open all day, from breakfast until late.

It is mostly walk-in, and after 6pm it only books groups of six or more, so we will expect a line. Waits can run 30 minutes to an hour, but they bring you chai while you wait and seat you at the bar first. On Fridays it stays open until midnight, which is exactly what the last night needs.

Friends of TGI on the Tee Sheet

A trip like this is better with locals. These are the British brands we have written about and would most like to play with while we are there. If that is you, the invite is open.

Three Putt

Graeme Whiles learned the game in his grandad’s field and runs Three Putt from West Yorkshire. He reached out to us first, so he gets the first text.

Parmore

“No Membership Required,” printed on half the range. That is the right attitude for a red-shirt round on Wimbledon Common.

Manors

The London brand Jojo Regan and Luke Davies started in 2019, and it’s on the rails at TrendyGolf. A round with them would be the trip’s home game.

Casualist

The West London label that started in Melbourne as Casual Pro. Pleated trousers at Walton Heath feel right.

Sounder

London knitwear people since 2021. If late April is cold on the heath, we know who to call.

Bordé

Tailored polos from St Albans, close enough to London to make The Addington.

Back home, this is what the TGI Social Club is for: small groups playing good courses around Austin, no dues.
