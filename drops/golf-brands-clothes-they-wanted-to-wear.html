Everyone Made the Golf Clothes They Wanted to Wear. Here’s What They Actually Made.

By Lenny Harrington · Updated October 12, 2026

Seventeen brands, one origin story33 picks · in stock, checked October 12, 2026
The TGI Take

Read enough About pages and you start to hear the same sentence. We couldn’t find golf clothes we wanted to wear, so we made them. It is the founding story of almost every independent golf brand we cover, and we have printed versions of it again and again on this site, most recently this week, in our Woodgrain post, before we noticed.

The sentence is true, which is why it keeps coming up. For decades the choice in a pro shop was a shiny performance polo with a big logo on the chest, and the people who started these brands were simply the ones who got fed up first. But the line tells you nothing about the brand. What does tell you something is what each founder decided they wanted.

So we went back through our own coverage and found seventeen brands with the same origin story. They split, almost neatly, into four answers. Some wanted something older: pleats, cable knits and the clothes from 1990s golf photos. Some wanted something louder: camo, prints and resort collars. Some wanted something from home: Harris Tweed, New Zealand wool, Supima cotton. And one just wanted a hat that fit. Below, each brand’s line in their words or ours, then two pieces that show what they actually made.

How to Read the Sentence

When a brand tells you it made what it wanted to wear, the useful question is: wanted instead of what? Woodgrain wanted the trousers from old tournament photos instead of a slim five-pocket. Olydoe wanted cotton instead of polyester. Fyfe wanted Scotland instead of mass-produced. Huega House wanted a hat that sat right on the head. The answer to that question is the brand. The sentence is just the doorway.

The Originals

The Questions

Why do so many golf brands say they made clothes they wanted to wear?

Because for decades golf clothing mostly meant shiny performance polos from a few big companies. Independent brands started when golfers who wanted something else, from knit headcovers and pleated trousers to streetwear and Harris Tweed, made it themselves.

Which golf brand on this list is the oldest?

Jan Craig Headcovers. Jan Craig started hand-knitting her own covers in 1962 because she could not find any she liked, and the covers are still hand-knit in the USA.

Which brands make golf clothes without polyester?

Olydoe calls itself an anti-polyester brand and makes Supima cotton polos and cotton, Tencel and linen trousers. Odd Ritual's Heritage Polo is a cotton pique, and Late Nine works in merino and Italian wool.

Which brands here make pleated golf trousers?

Woodgrain (the Legacy Double Pleated Trouser, $180), Late Nine (Double Pleat Slacks in Italian wool), Manors (the Lightweight Pleated Trouser, $172) and Olydoe (the Pleated Walker Pant, $115).

How much do these pieces cost?

On October 12, 2026 they ran from $22.50 (Smathers & Branson's needlepoint ball marker) to US $427 (Late Nine's wool slacks), with most between $50 and $200.
