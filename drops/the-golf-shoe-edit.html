The Golf Shoe Edit

13 Pieces

Golf shoes used to be simple — leather uppers, metal spikes, one colorway if you were lucky. Now they're the loudest piece in the bag. Sneaker culture crashed the fairway and didn't leave, and the best brands in golf are treating footwear like a canvas instead of an afterthought. This is our first shoe edit, and we pulled from every corner of the TGI universe to build it.

The lineup opens with Metalwood Studio's adidas MC70 — a Predator upper on a golf chassis — and runs through Quiet Golf's Samba, Eastside Golf's Jordan AJ1 Low, Malbon's in-house Tour 1, and three Nike retros that belong on a course as much as they do on concrete. PAYNTR shows up three times through collabs with Students Golf, Sugarloaf Social Club, and Vessel — each one engineered differently, each one carrying a different brand's DNA. Thirteen shoes, thirteen stories, $125 to $250.

Metalwood Studio
× adidas MC70 Golf · $200
Predator upper on a golf chassis. Silver metallic leather, BOOST midsole with LIGHTSTRIKE technology and Torsion system, water-resistant laces. Soccer-inspired asymmetry with deco-stitched toe detail — the kind of shoe that makes people ask questions on the first tee.

Quiet Golf
× Adidas Samba Golf · $150
The Samba — maybe the most iconic sneaker silhouette of the decade — gets a golf-ready sole and Quiet Golf's restrained touch. Spikeless traction, gum outsole, full-grain leather upper. The shoe that proved golf doesn't need to look like golf.

Eastside Golf
× Jordan AJ1 Low G · ~$180
Olajuwon Ajanaku and Earl Cooper brought Jordan to the course and changed the conversation. The AJ1 Low G keeps the Wings logo, the Jumpman tongue, and the silhouette that started it all — now with integrated traction for 18.

Malbon Golf
Tour 1 · $228
Malbon's first in-house golf shoe — not a collab, not a rebrand, built from scratch. Spikeless outsole with multi-directional traction, premium leather upper, Buckets logo on the heel. The shoe they've been building toward since day one.

Manors
× Reebok OG Pump Golf · $225
London's Manors brought the Pump to the course. Reebok's original inflation system for a custom fit, spikeless golf outsole, premium materials throughout. The kind of crossover that makes you stop and look twice on the first tee.

Students Golf

Students Golf and PAYNTR built a shoe with a magnetic ball marker embedded in the tongue — pull it off, mark your ball, snap it back. Full-grain leather, spikeless sole, and the kind of detail that separates a golf shoe from a statement.

Sugarloaf Social Club

Vessel
× PAYNTR SL · $229
Vessel's bag engineering meets PAYNTR's shoe engineering — a carbon fiber plate for energy return, premium leather upper, and the same obsessive material quality Vessel brings to their Player III bags. Performance and taste in equal measure.

PAYNTR's flagship — the shoe they build everything else around. Full-grain leather, removable spikes for tournament play, and a classic silhouette that nods to old-school golf without living in it. Three collabs on this list run through PAYNTR's DNA.

Nike
Air Max 1 '86 OG G · $160
Tinker Hatfield's 1987 original — the shoe that invented the visible Air window — rebuilt for the course. Waterproof upper, integrated spikeless traction, and a silhouette that hasn't lost a step in nearly four decades. The sneaker that started everything, now on grass.

Nike
Air Max 90 G · $145
The AM90 might be the most recognizable sneaker profile on earth — the infrared mudguard, the waffle outsole, the Max Air unit. Nike kept every detail and swapped the sole for integrated golf traction. Five colorways, zero compromise on the silhouette.

Nike
