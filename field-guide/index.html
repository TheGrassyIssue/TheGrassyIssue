A note on pricing

Here's the thing about Austin municipal golf: the four 18-hole munis all charge the same price. $35 Monday through Thursday, $41 Friday, $44 on weekends and holidays. $28 after the sunset cutoff. Which means when you're choosing between Lions, Morris Williams, Roy Kizer, and Jimmy Clay — price isn't the question. Character is.

The Municipal Courses.

Five public courses · The backbone of Austin golf
No. 01 · Since 1924
Lions Muni.
The historic one. Central. Short. The one you play first.
Best for: Walkers, visitors, good vibes

Lions plays like a neighborhood. Austin High kids practicing on the range, walkers carrying their own bags, a putting green complex at the clubhouse that's genuinely one of the best at any muni in the state. You feel the vibe before you tee off — pulling up on a Saturday morning, the place just feels like Austin.

The course itself is scorable in a way that rewards a regular. Front nine and back nine have distinct personalities — the transition between them is one of the nicest things about the round — and a run of pars and birdies feels earned rather than handed to you. At 6,001 yards par 71 from the tips, it's the shortest of the Austin 18-holers, which sounds like a feature until you try to keep a driver on the narrow tree-lined fairways.

Ben Crenshaw and Tom Kite learned here. One of the first courses in the South to desegregate, in 1951 — years before Brown v. Board. It sits on UT land that's been quietly fought over for a decade; "save the Muny" is a sincere Austin cause.

How to play it
  • Off the teeLeave the driver in the bag more than you think. Fairways are tight and tree-lined — a 3-wood or hybrid to the center of the fairway beats a 280-yard slice into the oaks every time.
  • ApproachThe greens are small and well-guarded by mature trees. Favor the fat part of the green rather than chasing pins. Miss to the center, not the edge.
  • TempoWalk it. Lions is the most walkable muni in the system — flat transitions, short distances between greens and tees. The pace is part of the experience.
  • Local tipSpend 20 minutes on the putting green before your round. The practice complex at Lions is genuinely one of the best at any muni in Texas, and the greens on the course roll the same speed.

No. 02 · Since 1964
Morris Williams.
The local's course. East side. Real character.
Best for: Regulars, real golfers, elevation junkies

Morris Williams is the most elevation you'll find on an Austin muni, and you feel it in your legs if you walk it — which you should. This is the one that plays like real golf: longer holes, better green complexes than anywhere else in the muni system, and a conditioning standard that doesn't apologize. Some of the best downtown skyline views too, from the back nine.

Leon Howard design from 1964, opened as Austin's third public course, home to the UT golf team during the Kite and Crenshaw years under coach George Hannon. At 7,023 yards par 72 from the Black tees it is both the longest and the toughest test in the muni system — small elevated greens, real contouring, rolling terrain that actually uses central Texas geography. Water runs through holes 11 to 14. The clubhouse has rocking chairs on the porch overlooking the course and is not trying to impress anyone, which is exactly the point.

How to play it
  • Off the teeDriver is fine on most holes, but the elevated greens will punish you if you're out of position. Pick a side and commit — there's usually a clear bail-out on every hole if you know where to look.
  • GreensThe green complexes are the best in the muni system. They're small, contoured, and elevated — expect more chips and pitches than usual. Dial in your 50-to-80-yard game before you come here.
  • Walk itIt's hilly, so cart riders miss half the fun. Walking Morris Williams is a workout, but the elevation changes are the whole personality of the course. Hydrate in summer.
  • Local tipPlay holes 11 through 14 with extra focus — water comes into play on all four and that stretch makes or breaks your card. Also: grab the rocking chairs on the clubhouse porch after the round.
#7
Signature hole · HCP 1
The blind tee shot that rewards trust.
Par 4 · 404 yards · Blind tee shot
The #1 handicap hole at Morris Williams — and the most "MW" hole on the course. You stand on the tee aiming at a ridge you can't see past. The ball disappears for three seconds before it reappears on the other side — or doesn't. The fairway narrows beyond the crest, trees pinch both sides, and the green sits below in a natural bowl. Trust the 3-wood, trust the line, walk up and see what the golf gods left you.

No. 03 · Since 1899
Hancock.
The oldest course in Texas. Nine holes. $20.
Best for: Casual rounds, beginners, 90-minute afternoons

Hancock is as casual as Austin golf gets — no need for a polo or anything. It's smack in the middle of a residential neighborhood so try not to hit a car. Weekends crawl, but on off times you can get around in under 90 minutes. Show up solo and jump in a group of random guys; it's always super fun. There's a tricky par 5 toward the end that'll surprise you the first time through.

Nine holes, $20 flat every day of the week, tucked into a central-Austin neighborhood that's been here since Reconstruction. Walking only. The oldest golf course in Texas, doing what it's done since 1899.

How to play it
  • Club choiceYou can play the whole course with 5 clubs — a hybrid, 7-iron, pitching wedge, sand wedge, and a putter. Don't overthink it. The holes are short but demand accuracy over distance.
  • PaceShow up solo or as a twosome on a weekday afternoon and you'll be through in 75 minutes. Weekend mornings back up — arrive early or wait until after 2pm.
  • HazardsCars parked along the street are in play on a couple holes. The neighborhood is the hazard. Hit it straight and you're fine; miss wide and you're apologizing to someone's windshield.
  • Local tipNo driving range, no warm-up area. You step up to the first tee cold. Stretch in the parking lot and hit your first swing with confidence — everyone who's played Hancock knows the feeling.
#1
Signature hole · HCP 1
The oldest first tee in Texas.
Par 4 · 324 yards · Opening hole
The #1 handicap hole on the card, and the oldest first tee in the state of Texas. At 324 yards it's technically drivable — oaks lining both sides of a narrow fairway say otherwise. No range, no warm-up swings. Just step up and hit. The green is small and slopes away. Locals will tell you a par on the first at Hancock is earned, not given. It's the hardest hole on a course that's been humbling people since 1899.

No. 04 · Since 1994
Roy Kizer.
Links-style. Flat. Cinematic at sunset.
Best for: Drivers of the ball, walkers, a clean test

Kizer feels different from every other muni in Austin — links-style, wide fairways, flat as a table. You can let the driver rip without thinking twice, and losing a ball here takes actual effort. Walking is the way to go. If you've been stuck at tight tree-lined courses for a month, Kizer is where you go to remember what a full swing feels like.

The 16th is the course's brain-twister — just outside driver distance, with a deep bunker short of the green that will keep you coming back until you figure out the right club. Play it on a Saturday during UT football season and you'll get the Longhorn flyover directly overhead, which is the kind of thing you can't plan for but never forget.

Randolph Russell links-style layout, opened 1994. 6,819 yards par 71 with 57 acres of lakes and wetlands threaded through the fairways. Named for the long-time Lions Muni superintendent who retired in 1973. Wind is the real hazard — don't come on a 25 mph day unless you want character-building. Come at 5:30pm in April with your phone in your pocket.

How to play it
  • Off the teeThis is your driver course. Wide fairways, minimal trees, room to miss. Grip it and rip it — you'll rarely be punished for length here, only for direction when water's in play.
  • WindThe number one variable at Kizer. The course is flat and exposed, so a 15 mph south wind turns a 150-yard approach into a 170-yard club. Check the forecast before you pick your tees.
  • Water57 acres of lakes and wetlands weave through the course. Know which side of the fairway the water is on before you tee off — the mistake isn't the water, it's not knowing it's there.
  • Local tipBook a sunset tee time in spring or fall. The light at Kizer between 5:30 and 7pm is unlike anything else in Austin golf. Also: the 16th is a brain-twister — just lay up short of that deep bunker.
#15
Signature hole · HCP 18
The prettiest par 3 you'll ever bogey.
Par 3 · 137 yards · Bunkers + wind
The easiest hole on the scorecard by handicap — and somehow half the foursome walks off with bogey. Only 137 yards, stock wedge territory, but the wind at Kizer always has something to say. Bunkers guard the left and right sides of the green. The green itself is wider than it looks from the tee but slopes in ways that turn a 30-footer into a three-putt. At sunset, it's the most photographed hole on the south side. Stick it close and you'll forget every bad shot that came before.

No. 05 · Since 1974
Jimmy Clay.
Longest of the munis. South. Underrated.
Best for: Sunset rounds, long hitters, south-siders

Jimmy Clay is the muni that's quietly overlooked, which is a shame because it holds up. A handful of fun holes on the front, and a back nine that's genuinely lovely — reason enough to drive south even on days when Kizer has tee times available. This is the course to play at sunset: big Texas sky, long shadows, the kind of light that makes even a bad shot feel like part of the round.

Joe Finger design from 1974, tucked along Williamson Creek on the south side of town, part of the 36-hole complex it shares with Roy Kizer. 6,931 yards of traditional tree-lined fairways, par 72, and the quietest tee sheet in the muni system. A 2007 renovation added a water feature and an island green; a second renovation in 2015 reshaped four greens.

How to play it
  • Off the teeAt 6,931 yards, Jimmy Clay rewards length. Play from the tees that match your game — this is the only Austin muni where the back tees can genuinely add a full hour to your round if you're not hitting it 250+.
  • Front nineA few forgettable holes mixed with a couple of genuinely fun ones. Don't let the front lull you — the back nine is where the course wakes up and the scoring windows close.
  • Back nineThis is the reason to play Jimmy Clay. The stretch from 12 through 16 is the best back-nine run in the muni system. Williamson Creek comes into play, the light gets good, and the holes have real character.
  • Local tipThis is the least crowded 18-hole muni in Austin. If you want a quiet round without a wait, book Jimmy Clay on a weekday afternoon. Pair it with Kizer for a 36-hole day — they share a parking lot.
#6
Signature hole · HCP 11
The island green you don't expect at a muni.
Par 4 · 316 yards · Near-island green
Part of the 2007 renovation that reshaped three holes and brought real water into play. At 316 from the blacks, it's a short par 4 — but the approach is the whole story. The green is nearly an island, surrounded by water on three sides. The tee shot is a positioning play; the second shot is the one that separates a birdie from a triple. Miss the green and you're fishing. Hit it and you feel like you pulled off something you had no business pulling off. The best surprise on the south side of town.

Special Day Courses

When you want something a step up.

The munis are the backbone of golf in Austin, but they're not the whole story. Within 30 minutes of downtown there are public and semi-private courses that feel like a different world — better conditioning, bigger budgets, designs by architects you'd recognize. These are the places you book for a birthday, a bachelor round, when friends visit from out of town, or when you just want a Saturday that feels like an occasion.

None of these replace the munis. They're additions — for the days when you want to dress a little nicer, pay a little more, and play something that was designed to impress. Prices range from $50 to $180+ depending on the day, so check rates before you book.

Falconhead Golf Club.
Public · Bee Cave · 25 min from downtown
The closest thing to a destination course within Austin's city limits. The par-3 8th sits on a limestone ledge cantilevered above a creek — one of the most beautiful holes in Texas. The rest of the layout is carved through Hill Country terrain with real elevation, mature oaks, and a level of conditioning that the munis can't match. Dynamic pricing means weekday afternoons can be surprisingly affordable; weekend mornings run north of $150. Excellent practice facilities.

Holes winding through limestone caverns, live oaks, streams, and ravines — this is what happens when you give a Jack Nicklaus associate natural Hill Country terrain to work with. Five par 3s and five par 5s give it a different rhythm than most courses, and the 7th is a signature hole you'll remember. The TopTracer-equipped driving range and Brushy Creek views from the bar make the whole trip feel like more than a round of golf. Conditioning is consistently excellent.

ShadowGlen Golf Club.
Public · Manor · 20 min from downtown
If you want the hardest public course in the Austin area, this is the one. A slope of 139 from the tips, 7,174 yards, and a back nine that uses elevation changes more aggressively than anything in the muni system. The front nine plays more links-like; the back tightens through trees and drops. Reviewers consistently mention the drive-thru bar at the turn. Despite the difficulty, it's surprisingly accessible — the five tee sets range from 4,898 to 7,174 yards, so there's a setup for everyone. One of Austin's genuine hidden gems.

Omni Barton Creek Resort.
Resort · West Austin · 20 min from downtown
The big one. Four 18-hole championship courses at a single resort — Fazio Canyons (ranked top 35 in Golf Digest), Fazio Foothills, Crenshaw Cliffside, and Palmer Lakeside. Practice & Ranges

Where to sharpen your game.

Austin has better practice infrastructure than most cities its size. Driving ranges with real grass tees, dedicated short game areas, and a growing number of indoor sim spots for when summer heat hits triple digits or winter rain rolls through. If you want to work on your game without committing to a full round, you have options.

The practice scene has gotten surprisingly good in the last few years. New facilities have opened on the south side, established ranges have upgraded their tech, and the indoor sim market has exploded. Whether you need an hour on the range or a full swing analysis session, it's covered.

Beyond the City

When you want something different.

The Hill Country and surrounding area have courses that are a completely different experience from the munis — resort-style layouts, Hill Country views, and designs from architects whose names you'd recognize. These aren't replacements for the munis — they're additions. When you want elevation changes that make Morris Williams look flat, or fairways carved through live oak canyons, or the feeling of playing somewhere that was designed to be a destination, this is the list.

Butler Pitch & Putt.
Public · Par 3 · Downtown

Nine par-3 holes downtown on the south shore of Lady Bird Lake, walk-up rates, a beer stand at the turn, and the Austin skyline as your backdrop. Not a serious test of golf — closer to a first date or a weeknight hang than a real round. But it's an Austin institution, and it's the correct answer to "where can I play in jeans right now without a tee time." Bring a few clubs, grab a beer, and enjoy the sunset. One of the best casual golf experiences in any city.

Joe Balander Short Course.
Part of the Kizer complex · Par 3 practice

A nine-hole par-3 practice course at the Roy Kizer / Jimmy Clay complex. $5 adults, $3 juniors. Under the radar, open to the public, good for dialing in your wedges or teaching a beginner without the pressure of a full round. Walk only.

Vaaler Creek Golf Club.
Public · Blanco · 50 min from Austin

The Hill Country day trip. Ranked #10 in Texas by the Dallas Morning News, Vaaler Creek is in Blanco — about 50 minutes west of Austin. Michael Lowry design with 6,946 yards of beautifully conditioned fairways, a restored 1860s ranch house for a clubhouse, and a shaded deck overlooking the 18th. Not close enough to be a regular play, but absolutely a reason to get in the car for a Saturday morning.

ColoVista Golf Club.
Public · Bastrop · 35 min from Austin

A wild ride of a course in Bastrop overlooking the Colorado River. The signature 15th — a 191-yard par 3 off a cliff with a 200-foot drop to the green — is one of the most dramatic holes in central Texas. The front nine plays links-style, the back nine uses extreme elevation through rugged terrain. Ranked #1 Hidden Gem by the Houston Chronicle. Check recent reviews for condition updates.

Harvey Penick Golf Campus.
Public · 9 holes · Northeast Austin

Nine holes at par 30 — three par 4s and six par 3s — on 112 acres off Ed Bluestein, crossing Walnut Creek three times. It reads like a sixth muni but it is not one: the campus is owned and run by First Tee – Greater Austin, a non-profit, on land leased from the East Communities YMCA. It opened in 2005. Eighteen dollars to walk Monday through Thursday, $20 Friday through Sunday and holidays, and seniors 55 and over walk for $13 on weekdays only. Juniors are $10. GolfATX members and City of Austin employees pay $5. There is also a grass-only range, a three-hole short course, and an 18-hole FootGolf course — the first in Austin. About a two-mile walk and ninety minutes.

Riverside Golf Course.
Public · Southeast Austin · 10 min from downtown

The most under-appreciated piece of golf history in the city. Riverside is a Perry Maxwell design — built with his son Press, announced in the Statesman in March 1947, open for play by 1949 — and it was Austin Country Club's second home, from 1949 until the club moved to the Pete Dye course at Davenport Ranch in 1984. Which means two of the three ACC sites are now public golf you can walk on: Hancock was the first, Riverside the second. Eighteen holes, par 72, 6,359 from the blue tees. Thirty-three dollars to walk on a weekday morning and $21 after 4pm. The land is owned today by Austin Community College.

Plum Creek Golf Course.
Public · Kyle · 30 min south

Par 71 and just over 7,100 yards from the black tees, which makes it one of the longest cards near Austin. Toptracer range, three acres of short game, and it is the home course for Texas State's men's and women's teams. Pricing is dynamic and starts at $70 Monday to Thursday and $100 Friday to Sunday, with a super-twilight rate that drops to $35 after 5pm on weekdays. Book plumcreekgolf.com — note that plumcreekgolfclub.com is an entirely different club.

Forest Creek Golf Club.
Public · Round Rock · under 30 min north

A Dick Phelps design from 1990, owned by the City of Round Rock and run by KemperSports — a muni under private management. At 7,147 yards from the Gold tees with a 139 slope it is the longest and hardest course in this guide, munis included. There is no rate card at all; pricing moves with demand and the tee sheet opens 30 days out, so expect anywhere from about $40 on a twilight weekday to $100 for a weekend morning. Booking early genuinely costs less.

Kissing Tree Golf Club.
Semi-private · San Marcos · 45 min south

A Gary Stephenson design that opened in October 2018, managed by Troon, certified as an Audubon Signature Sanctuary and irrigated entirely with reclaimed water. Par 72, 6,572 from the back. The thing to know: the surrounding community is 55-and-over, but the golf course is not — Kissing Tree runs a separate public tee sheet alongside resident play, and anyone can book it. Public 18s ran $93 to $127 through late August. Read the rules before you go: no non-playing riders, two players per cart, no private carts unless you live there, and check in no more than sixty minutes before your time.

Frequently Asked

Common questions about golf in Austin.

Where can I play golf in Austin?

Austin has five municipal golf courses open to the public: Lions Municipal, Morris Williams, Hancock, Roy Kizer, and Jimmy Clay. Green fees range from $20 to $44, making them the most affordable option in the city. Beyond the munis, there's the semi-private Grey Rock Golf Club, the par-3 Butler Pitch and Putt downtown, and a handful of private clubs. For most people, the munis are where it starts.

How much does it cost to play golf in Austin?

The four 18-hole municipal courses all charge the same rate: $35 Monday through Thursday, $41 on Friday, and $44 on weekends and holidays. Sunset rates drop to $28 at all four. Hancock, the 9-hole course, is $20 flat every day of the week. These are the most affordable 18-hole options in the city — and the pricing is identical across Lions, Morris Williams, Roy Kizer, and Jimmy Clay.

What is the best public golf course in Austin?

It depends on what you're looking for. Morris Williams is the toughest test with the best green complexes and real elevation. Lions Municipal is the most historic and beloved — Ben Crenshaw and Tom Kite grew up here. Roy Kizer is a wide-open links-style layout, great for big hitters. Morris Williams is also the longest at 7,023 yards. Jimmy Clay is the least crowded. And Hancock is a $20, 9-hole neighborhood classic and one of the oldest courses in Texas.

Are there indoor golf simulators in Austin?

Yes — and the scene is growing fast. Austin has seven-plus indoor simulator venues across town, equipped with TrackMan, Foresight, and Full Swing systems. Options range from standalone sim bays to full bar-and-lounge setups with leagues and events. We've reviewed all of them in our simulator guide.

What should I do after a round of golf in Austin?

Austin's post-round scene is one of the best in the country. Hit a BBQ joint near whatever course you just played — we've mapped 12 spots by proximity. Grab a smash burger, head to a patio bar, or check out our full list of post-round moves for swimming holes, live music, and more.

Where should I play for a special occasion in Austin?

For a birthday or celebration, Falconhead in Bee Cave is the go-to — its par-3 8th on a limestone cliff is unforgettable. For a bachelor group, Star Ranch in Hutto offers an all-inclusive package with green fees, cart, range, and meals. For the full resort experience, Omni Barton Creek has four Tom Fazio, Crenshaw, and Palmer courses at one property. For the toughest public test, ShadowGlen in Manor has a slope of 139 from the tips. All are within 30 minutes of downtown. See our full special day section for eight courses.

How do I play the Austin munis well?

Each muni rewards a different approach. At Lions, leave the driver in the bag — it's short and tight, so accuracy beats distance. At Morris Williams, bring your short game — the elevated greens are the best in the system. At Kizer, rip the driver but respect the wind. At Jimmy Clay, play the right tees and save energy for the back nine. At Hancock, bring five clubs and enjoy the walk. We've added detailed "How to Play It" strategy tips to every course in the municipal section.

Is Austin good for golf?

Austin is one of the best muni golf cities in the country. Five affordable public courses with real character, eight-plus practice facilities, a growing indoor simulator scene, 300-plus days of playable weather, and a food and bar culture that makes the post-round as good as the round itself. Whether you're a beginner or a single-digit handicap, there's something here for you.

What are the best golf courses in Austin?

For everyday golf, the five munis — Lions, Morris Williams, Hancock, Roy Kizer, and Jimmy Clay — are the backbone of Austin golf, all between $20 and $44. For a step up, our special-day section covers the area's semi-private and upscale daily-fee courses. And if you're willing to drive, some of Texas's best public golf is within a few hours — covered in our Texas road-trip list and the Austin Golf Road Trip.

What is the cheapest golf in Austin?

Hancock — $20 flat, every day, for the oldest course in Texas (1899), walking only. Next best value: sunset rates at the four 18-hole munis drop to $28. Butler Pitch and Putt downtown is the budget short-game option, and range sessions across the city's practice facilities start around $10 a bucket.

Is Austin a top golf city?

GOLF Magazine has called Austin the best golf city in America, and the case is easy to make: year-round playability, five public munis with shared history back to Ben Crenshaw and Tom Kite's junior days, a booming practice-and-simulator scene, and the best post-round food in the sport. This guide exists because the city's golf culture deserves the documentation.
