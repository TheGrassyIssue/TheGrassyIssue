Our 25 Best Independent Golf Brands

These are the twenty-five independent golf brands The Grassy Issue has written about most. We ranked them on the record, not on a gut call: every page on the site that mentions a brand counts once, and the brands with the most pages sit at the top. Manors Golf leads with 34 posts, followed by Students Golf and Devereux Golf.

Each brand has its own page in the Brand Index, which links out to every post that counts toward its number. Read the list as a map of where our attention has actually gone in 2026: the brands we keep going back to for drops, gift guides, roundups and field notes.

Looking for more than twenty-five? See every independent golf brand in the Index, or browse the full golf brand directory.

How this list works

These are the brands we keep coming back to because they make good stuff, they are consistent about it, and they have a clear point of view. Every one is still run by the people who started it, or by a family, rather than by a parent company. Most are small, and a few have brought in outside partners along the way.

The order comes from our own coverage: every TGI page that mentions a brand counts once, and the brands we have written about most sit at the top. We last checked who runs each one on October 5, 2026. The list updates whenever the Brand Index is rebuilt, so it moves as we write.

  1. 01

    Manors Golf

    34 posts · London, UK · apparel

    Manors started in London in 2019 arguing that golf did not need technical clothing, then relaunched in 2023 as a technical brand without changing how anything looks. The result is modern fabric, like Polartec polos and recycled trousers, cut to a silhouette borrowed from golf's golden age.

    Founded in London in 2019 by school friends Jojo Regan and Luke Davies, who still run it; a 2026 minority round was led by Redrice Ventures.

  2. 02

    Students Golf

    24 posts · USA · apparel

    Students filters streetwear, campus culture and vintage Americana through golf: the Calculus pants, the Librarian polo, the 1st String sweater. It is golf clothing from people who grew up on skate videos and college football Saturdays, with a deep bench of collaborations behind it.

    Founded in 2021 by Michael Huynh with business partner Bryan Lowman.

  3. 03

    Devereux Golf

    20 posts · Scottsdale, AZ · apparel

    Devereux is an apparel brand that happens to be famous for its leather headcovers. Robert Brunner left the gas industry for a year at fashion school, came back with a golf line and brought in his brother Will; the clothes sit closer to workwear than a golf shell, and are fine off the course.

    Founded in 2013 by brothers Robert and Will Brunner, and still family-run.

  4. 04

    Jones Sports Co

    19 posts · Portland, OR · bags

    Jones has made the same carry bag since 1971, and the restraint is the product: no legs, no cart strap, a light bag with a three-way divider. When the current owners revived it in 2011 there were no patterns left, so they rebuilt the shape from bags borrowed off old customers.

    Family-owned: the Lemman family bought it in 2011, and Dean Lemman runs it as CEO.

  5. 05

    Sentinel Golf

    19 posts · Minneapolis, MN · bags

    Sentinel is a Minneapolis design lab that builds golf bags the way an outdoor brand builds a tent, with Dyneema, Danish leather and makers from well outside golf. John Mooty sells on limited pre-order with no restocks and no discounts, so the best colours go quickly.

    John Mooty designs and runs it from Minneapolis, selling mostly by pre-order.

  6. 06

    Birds of Condor

    18 posts · Byron Bay, Australia · apparel, headwear

    Birds of Condor is funny on purpose and made properly anyway. Frankie and Zoe Kimpton started with ten hats out of a Byron Bay garage in 2016, and their snapbacks for country clubs that do not exist were shipping to Japan, South Korea and the US within months.

    Founded in 2016 in Byron Bay by Frankie and Zoe Kimpton.

  7. 07

    Seamus Golf

    17 posts · Beaverton, OR · headcovers, accessories

    Seamus cuts and sews in its own Beaverton workshop, which is why it can work with registered Irish tartans and Pendleton wool at all. Akbar and Megan Chisti own it outright, and the hand-forged ball markers are the easiest way in before a headcover.

    Founded in 2011 by Akbar and Megan Chisti, who own the business outright.

  8. 08

    Gumtree Golf & Nature Club

    16 posts · Brooklyn, New York · headcovers, apparel, bags, accessories

    Gumtree works out of a Brooklyn studio, sewing headcovers, bags and clothes from deadstock, vintage and upcycled textiles, some of them wetsuit neoprene. Almost everything is made in New York or Los Angeles, and the brand also runs ecology retreats in the Catskills.

    Founder Karsten Jurkschat has said the brand is self-funded with no investors.

  9. 09

    Metalwood Studio

    14 posts · Los Angeles, CA · apparel, accessories

    Metalwood is art-directed first, and Cole Young has built the most consistent visual identity in independent golf out of medieval heraldry and industrial signage. An adidas partnership and a store on La Brea Avenue show how far that point of view has travelled.

    Founder Cole Young has said he is still the only person on the cap table.

  10. 10

    Fyfe Golf

    12 posts · UK · accessories, headcovers

    Fyfe began as Neil Rennie's complaint that Scottish pro shops sold nothing to do with Scotland. Its Fife workshop makes headcovers to order in Harris Tweed, Scottish tartan and Dundee waxed canvas, and a few times a year in upcycled RAF flight suits, caddie coveralls or yacht sails.

    Founded in Edinburgh by Neil Rennie; the UK company register lists a single controlling owner.

  11. 11

    VESSEL

    12 posts · Carlsbad, California · bags, headcovers, accessories

    VESSEL has made premium bags and headcovers out of Carlsbad since Ronnie Shaw founded it in 2013, and he still runs it. Its annual Masters-week limited edition is the one to watch: three of this year's five green croc-texture pieces were already gone when we wrote about it.

    Founded in 2013 by Ronnie Shaw, who still runs it as CEO.

  12. 12

    Random Golf Club

    11 posts · Los Angeles, CA · apparel, community

    Random Golf Club is a community that happens to run a pro shop. Erik Anders Lang and Evan Roosevelt started it to change who golf is for; the first meetup was eleven strangers in Melbourne, and it now counts 30,000 members across 140 chapters, with the events as the real product.

    Founded in 2020 by Erik Anders Lang and privately held with no outside backing.

  13. 13

    Bettinardi Golf

    10 posts · Tinley Park, Illinois · equipment, headcovers

    Bettinardi is a family business in a field of corporations, milling putters in Tinley Park, Illinois, since 1998 and still run by Bob and Sam Bettinardi. It also builds wedges to order, which is how it turned up in our custom wedge report.

    Family-owned since 1998 and run by Bob and Sam Bettinardi in Tinley Park, Illinois.

  14. 14

    Ghost Golf

    10 posts · Irvine, California · bags, accessories, apparel

    Ghost Golf was founded in Irvine in 2020 by Andy Nalla, who still runs it, and its bags are built around a patented magnetic towel. The Anyday line, including the Kovert Ops, brings a tactical edge to the stand bag.

    Founded in 2020 by Andy Nalla, who still runs it as CEO.

  15. 15

    MacKenzie Golf Bags

    9 posts · Oregon, USA · bags

    MacKenzie is named after a caddie, not a founder: Rick MacKenzie of St Andrews, who once tipped the Jacobsen brothers' staff bags into one leather pencil bag. The Oregon workshop has made carry bags by hand since 1985, and builds collaborations for Fyfe, Sugarloaf and Bandon Dunes.

    Family-owned: father and son Tom and Nic Mulflur run it in Oregon.

  16. 16

    Mogshade

    9 posts · Portugal · headcovers, accessories

    Mogshade makes everything to order in Portugal, so nothing is produced until someone buys it. The fabrics are natural, recycled or rescued, including wool from the Serra da Estrela mountain mills, and the name is an old dialect word for light and shadow moving through leaves.

    A small Lisbon company, Mogshade Golf Lda, co-founded by Nicola Mulinaris.

  17. 17

    Radry Golf

    9 posts · Sheboygan, WI · headwear, apparel

    Radry is one Wisconsin artist, Tony Knapton, and his logo is the tell: one eye a circle for birdies, the other a square for bogeys, and a wave for the up and down of a round. The runs are numbered, so the good ones do not come back.

    Tony Knapton's one-artist brand, run out of Sheboygan, Wisconsin.

  18. 18

    Sunday Golf

    9 posts · San Diego, California · bags, headcovers, accessories

    Sunday Golf makes lightweight carry bags for the par three and the casual nine, out of San Diego. Ronan Galvin and Casey Mathews founded it in 2020 on crowdfunded seed money, and the bags carry touches like a pocket built to keep a drink cold.

    Founded in 2020 by Ronan Galvin and Casey Mathews; its seed money came from crowdfunding.

  19. 19

    Walker Golf Things

    9 posts · Gold Coast, Australia · apparel, headwear

    Walker Golf Things draws everything by hand, out of the Gold Coast. The label was started by Glenn Walker, pro skater Jack Fardell and friends, and has collaborated with adidas, Wahine and Coopers.

    Started on the Gold Coast by Glenn Walker, pro skater Jack Fardell and friends; a private Australian company.

  20. 20

    Kingfisher Golf

    8 posts · Dallas, TX · apparel

    Kingfisher treats a shirt like a design brief, which follows from Fiona Cohen's years as an art director at PepsiCo. The graphics are drawn carefully and printed simply, some at Lewellyn's in Old East Dallas, and the brand signed its first athlete through a Worst Golfer in Dallas contest.

    Founded and run by Fiona Cohen in Dallas.

    Left of Field Golf

    7 posts · Sydney, Australia · apparel

    Left of Field is the funniest brand in golf that also makes clothes you would wear without the joke. Nick Ilias started it in his brother's old bedroom in Sydney in 2022, and every collection is named after a stretch of the Australian coast.

    Founded in 2022 by Nick Ilias in Sydney.

  22. 22

    Sounder

    7 posts · London, United Kingdom · apparel, accessories

    Sounder makes the golf clothes a well-dressed person already owns: cotton polos, good knitwear, a decent chino. It comes from the founders of Folk and Urban Golf in London, is named for Seve's first clubs, and the knitwear is the reason to know the name.

    Founded in London in 2020 by Cathal McAteer and James Day, the two people the UK register lists as controlling it.

  23. 23

    Pins & Aces

    6 posts · Arvada, Colorado · headcovers, apparel, accessories

    Pins & Aces is family-owned in Colorado, started in 2018 by brothers-in-law Nick Mertz and Jon Major, and embroiders its graphic headcovers in house. It holds a real Coca-Cola licence, which is how a soft-drink logo ends up on a ball marker.

    Founded in 2018 by brothers-in-law Nick Mertz and Jon Major in Colorado, with no private equity.

    Casualist

    5 posts · London, UK · apparel

    Casualist is Elie Reboul's one-founder label, started in Melbourne and now in West London, and it grew out of the name Casual Pro. The polos are made in Portugal, cut closer than an American golf polo and plain enough to wear away from the course.

    Elie Reboul's one-founder brand, started in Melbourne and now in London.

  25. 25

    Fella Golf

    5 posts · Europe · apparel, accessories

    Fella is an Amsterdam label with no interest in seriousness: heavyweight tees and a pizza-slice ball marker. It now sells to the US in dollars without Dutch VAT and ships by DHL Express in two to three business days.

    Behdad Rahnamai's Amsterdam company, Fella Golf B.V.
