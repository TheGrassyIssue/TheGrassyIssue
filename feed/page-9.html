Lions Municipal Golf Course
The Muny Is Getting
a Coore & Crenshaw
Renovation
Austin's oldest public course — and one of the most important in American golf — is about to get a world-class restoration. Here's what's coming.
The History
1924
Austin's first public course
In 1950, Lions became the first municipal course south of the Mason-Dixon line to desegregate — when two young Black caddies walked on and Mayor Taylor Glass said "Let them play." It stayed the only integrated muni in the South until 1953.
The Architects
Bill Coore &
Ben Crenshaw
The firm behind Pinehurst No. 2, Sand Hills, Sheep Ranch, and Cabot Cliffs. Crenshaw grew up playing Lions. Both are doing the renovation pro bono.
The Course Plan
Restore the Original
Pre-1974 Routing
141 acres returned to the layout that was intact from the course's golden era. The 1974 redesign altered the routing — Coore & Crenshaw will bring it back to what Crenshaw himself grew up playing.
Beyond Golf
New Clubhouse.
Restaurant. Trails.
Community Space.
New clubhouse & pro shop
Restaurant & event spaces
Expanded driving range
Trails to Town Lake
Farmers market & music
The Teaching Center
Honoring the
Integration of 1950
The historic clubhouse becomes a teaching center, golf museum, and civil rights education space. The very room where the call to Mayor Glass was placed will tell the story of what happened here.
Where Things Stand
2027
Current preservation deadline
The Muny Conservancy is working with the City and UT to secure the land permanently. The Imagine Muny gala has raised over $1M annually. The preservation district was extended to May 2027.
Support the Muny
